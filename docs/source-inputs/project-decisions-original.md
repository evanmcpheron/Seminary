\# 4. Treatment of opposing views and critical scholarship

A high-quality biblical scholar must understand arguments from atheists, agnostics, other religions, Roman Catholics, secular historians, historical-critical scholars, and theologians outside evangelical Protestantism.

Avoiding these sources entirely would produce weak scholarship and weak apologetics.

I recommend the following rule:

\> The curriculum is confessionally Protestant and will not advocate abandonment of Christianity, ridicule faith, or treat Christian belief as an intellectual defect. It will nevertheless present important opposing arguments accurately and in their strongest responsible form. Such positions will be studied for understanding and critically evaluated from the curriculum’s declared Christian and Protestant commitments.

This means, for example:

\* A course may assign Bart Ehrman, but will not treat his conclusions as unquestionable.

\* A course may assign Roman Catholic, Jewish, Muslim, atheist, or historical-critical authors.

\* Their views must be represented accurately.

\* The curriculum must distinguish description from endorsement.

\* A response must engage evidence and arguments rather than dismissing a position merely because it is non-Protestant.

\* Devotional formation and critical academic analysis may coexist.
