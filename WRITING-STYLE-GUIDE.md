# Writing Style Guide

## Governing style

Student papers use the **SBL Handbook of Style** for biblical studies, with Chicago notes-and-bibliography conventions where SBL defers to Chicago. Student formatting should be compatible with Turabian. MLA is not the default because SBL/Chicago is better aligned with biblical and theological scholarship.

## Canonical format

- UTF-8 Markdown with YAML front matter.
- Pandoc-compatible footnotes.
- DOCX and PDF are derivative exports, not canonical source files.
- American English.
- Greek and Hebrew in Unicode.
- Transliteration according to SBL conventions.

## Normal paper structure

1. Title and metadata.
2. Abstract when required.
3. Introduction.
4. Clear thesis statement.
5. Organized argument with meaningful headings.
6. Primary-source analysis.
7. Engagement with relevant scholarship.
8. Serious counterargument or alternative interpretation.
9. Confessional/theological evaluation where appropriate.
10. Conclusion.
11. Footnotes.
12. Bibliography.
13. Appendices when needed.

## Citation rules

- No unsupported factual or interpretive claims.
- No fabricated quotations, citations, page numbers, or bibliographic records.
- Direct quotations include exact page numbers or verified location markers.
- Do not cite a secondary source that has not been accessed.
- Identify the Bible translation at first citation and in metadata.
- Distinguish translations from the original languages.
- Cite primary texts according to discipline-specific conventions.

## Bible translation policy

- Primary English reading text: ESV.
- Required academic comparison: NASB 2020.
- Regular additional comparison: NIV.
- NLT may be used for light reading or readability comparison, but not as the sole basis of close exegesis.
- Other academic translations may be consulted when a course objective requires them.

These translation preferences never create a Bible-purchase requirement.
Required Scripture readings must use the verified lawful free access specified
by `SOURCE-POLICY.md`; any student-selected Bible purchase is optional.

## Original-language conventions

- Greek pronunciation: reconstructed Koine as the primary instructional model; students should recognize common Erasmian equivalents.
- Hebrew pronunciation: modern Israeli/Sephardic classroom pronunciation, with explicit study of Masoretic pointing and historical phonology where relevant.
- Language exercises include audio recitation and oral parsing when practical.

## Student authorship

The student must write the substantive prose of all graded work. AI feedback after a genuine student draft is permitted under `AI-USE-POLICY.md`. Every major assignment includes an AI-use declaration.
