# Academic Standards

## Credit-hour model

The project uses approximately 45 total hours of student work per credit hour.

| Credits | Total work | Average in a 15-week term |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 45 hours | 3 hours/week |
| 2 | 90 hours | 6 hours/week |
| 3 | 135 hours | 9 hours/week |
| 4 | 180 hours | 12 hours/week |

A normal term contains 14 instructional weeks and one final-examination or final-project week. January and summer terms may be compressed, but the total expected workload must remain comparable.

Undergraduate overloads are redistributed into summer terms when needed. Courses may be moved within the four undergraduate years to satisfy prerequisites, but may not be deleted or silently altered. Every move requires a recorded rationale in `curriculum/schedule-overrides.yaml`.

## Instructional pattern

- Standard courses: normally two substantial written lectures per instructional week plus guided work.
- Biblical languages: normally three or four shorter lessons per week, with frequent parsing, translation, vocabulary, audio recitation, and oral work.
- PhD seminars: research discussion, primary-source analysis, historiography, methods, seminar papers, and colloquia rather than artificial lecture volume.
- Dissertation years: research milestones, committee review, chapter production, and defense preparation rather than weekly lesson folders.
- Videos and podcasts supplement written instruction; they do not ordinarily replace it.

## Lecture standard

Each written lecture includes:

1. Title and metadata.
2. Learning objectives.
3. Key terms.
4. Required preparation.
5. Concise outline.
6. Full lecture text.
7. Primary-source engagement.
8. Major scholarly positions.
9. Confessional analysis.
10. Scripture references.
11. Discussion and retrieval questions.
12. Summary.
13. Further reading.
14. Formal citations and bibliography entries.
15. Claims requiring verification.
16. Revision date and provenance.

Typical ranges are 2,500–4,000 words for introductory undergraduate lectures, 3,500–5,500 for advanced undergraduate lectures, and 4,000–6,500 for MDiv lectures. Length follows pedagogical need rather than filler. Doctoral seminars use lectures sparingly.

## Progression thresholds

- Undergraduate progression: minimum course grade of C.
- Graduate progression: minimum course grade of B.
- Greek and Hebrew progression into advanced exegesis: minimum grade of B and demonstrated competence.
- One structured retake is permitted after assigned remediation.
- A failed prerequisite blocks dependent courses from being marked complete.

## Human evaluation schedule

- Years 1–3: no routine external mentor is required; human review may be sought sparingly for writing or language problems.
- Year 4: at least one human review of the senior capstone is strongly recommended.
- MDiv years: human pastoral, preaching, ministry, and selected language evaluation becomes required.
- PhD years: qualified human supervision, comprehensive-exam review, dissertation supervision, and at least two additional readers are required for completion claims.

Human evaluation forms live in `templates/evaluation/` and signed completion records belong in the relevant portfolio. Sensitive details must not be committed.
