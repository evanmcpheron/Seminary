# Theological Commitments

## Primary confession

This curriculum is broadly evangelical and Protestant. It receives historic Christianity as true and theology as disciplined study of reality under God, not merely detached description of religious behavior.

It affirms:

- The one true and living God, eternally existing as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
- The Apostles' Creed, Nicene Creed, and Chalcedonian Definition as faithful summaries of central Christian doctrine.
- The full deity and full humanity of Jesus Christ.
- Christ's atoning work, bodily resurrection, ascension, present reign, and promised return.
- Salvation by God's grace through faith in Jesus Christ.
- The authority, truthfulness, inspiration, and inerrancy of Holy Scripture, substantially aligned with the Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy.
- The 66-book Protestant canon as canonical Scripture.
- The Apocrypha/Deuterocanonical books as historically and theologically important but not canonical within this curriculum.
- The historic Protestant principles of the Reformation.
- The necessity of the local church, worship, prayer, discipleship, mission, Christian moral formation, and love of neighbor.

## Constructive Protestant framework

The curriculum is broadly evangelical rather than bound to one denomination. Reformed, Baptist, Anglican, Lutheran, Wesleyan/Methodist, and Pentecostal/Charismatic traditions must be represented accurately on secondary questions.

The following are normally treated comparatively:

- Calvinism and Arminianism.
- Credobaptism and paedobaptism.
- Protestant understandings of the Lord's Supper.
- Continuationism and cessationism.
- Episcopal, presbyterian, and congregational church government.
- Major orthodox Christian approaches to creation and evolution.
- Historic premillennial, dispensational, amillennial, and postmillennial eschatology.
- Covenantal, dispensational, and progressive-covenantal views of Israel and the church.
- Christian political theology without binding the curriculum to a political party.

## Pastoral office

The curriculum must present complementarian and egalitarian biblical arguments accurately and charitably. Its constructive ministry default is complementarian: the ordained pastor/elder office is reserved for biblically qualified men. This position must not be used to demean women, erase their scholarship, or minimize the breadth and importance of women's service, teaching, theological work, discipleship, and ministry within the church.

## Critical and non-Christian scholarship

Atheist, agnostic, Jewish, Muslim, Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, secular, historical-critical, and other non-evangelical scholarship may be assigned when academically relevant. Such work must be represented in its strongest responsible form. Description is not endorsement. Evaluation must address evidence and argument rather than dismissing a position merely because it is non-Protestant.

The curriculum must not advocate abandonment of Christianity, ridicule faith, or treat belief as an intellectual defect. It must also not protect the student from serious evidence, difficult questions, or responsible criticism. Devotional formation and rigorous academic analysis belong together.
