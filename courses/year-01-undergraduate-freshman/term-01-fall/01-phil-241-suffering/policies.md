# Course Policies

This course inherits all repository-level policies. The root policy governs if a
course-level summary below is less specific or appears to conflict.

## Independent and non-accredited status

PHIL 241 is part of an independent, non-accredited curriculum. It is not an
official Wheaton College course or syllabus and is not produced, taught,
approved, accredited, sponsored, or endorsed by Wheaton College. Completion
confers no academic credit, degree progress, admission standing, ordination,
licensure, or certification.

## Academic integrity and student authorship

The student's submitted prose, argument analysis, oral answers, and examination
responses must be the student's own. Sources must be acknowledged using SBL
style with Chicago notes-and-bibliography conventions where SBL defers to
Chicago. Fabricated quotations, citations, pagination, bibliographic records, or
access claims are prohibited.

The argument reconstruction brief, comparative analysis paper, oral defense
dialogue, and final integrative examination require an AI-use declaration. AI
may clarify instructions, tutor concepts, generate ungraded practice, locate
candidate sources for student verification, and critique a genuine student
draft. AI may not draft submitted prose, supply oral answers, complete source
analysis, or answer graded quizzes or examinations.

## Source access and copyright

Use purchased, library, publisher-hosted, public-domain, or otherwise licensed
copies. Do not commit copyrighted books, articles, scans, answer keys, or media
transcripts to the repository. Direct quotations require a verified location in
the edition actually used. The bibliography deliberately omits anthology page
ranges until they are checked against the acquired edition.

## Confessional and academic engagement

The course works constructively within the repository's broadly evangelical
Protestant commitments. Confessional commitment does not excuse weak argument,
selective evidence, caricature, or manufactured certainty. Atheist, agnostic,
non-Christian, Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, historical-critical, and other
perspectives must be presented in their strongest responsible form before
evaluation. Description does not imply endorsement; disagreement does not imply
ridicule.

Important claims should distinguish primary-source evidence, broad scholarly
consensus, contested interpretation, confessional conclusion, and curriculum
synthesis. The 66-book Protestant canon is the course's canonical Scripture.
The ESV is the primary English text and NASB 2020 is required for comparison.

## Difficult content, privacy, and pastoral limits

The course discusses death, grief, disability, illness, abuse, violence,
atrocity, animal suffering, divine hiddenness, and challenges to Christian
belief. The student is never required to disclose personal trauma, counseling
history, confidential pastoral information, or another person's private story.
Synthetic or public cases must be available for every case-based activity.

Philosophical argument is not pastoral care. Academic exercises may assess
listening, non-coercive language, lament, referral judgment, and safeguarding
awareness, but they do not authorize diagnosis, therapy, legal advice, or work
beyond competence. Immediate safety, abuse, mandatory-reporting, or mental-health
concerns require appropriate local emergency, safeguarding, pastoral, or
licensed professional support. Private details must not be committed to Git.

## Assessment, grading, and retake

Every graded assessment maps to published learning outcomes and must receive a
rubric or scoring standard before it is assigned. Student-facing tasks and
instructor-only answer standards remain separate. Oral work should be recorded
when practical.

The undergraduate progression threshold is C (73%). One structured retake may
be offered only after required remediation. The course record must retain the
original result, remediation completed, retake result, and final progression
decision. Assessment standards may not be changed retroactively to secure a
passing result.

## Accessibility and accommodations

Reasonable accommodations should be documented before the affected work when
possible and may adjust format, timing, or access without reducing an essential
learning outcome. Accessible alternatives must preserve the same outcome and
comparable workload. A transcript-supported media option is available for the
required video; a non-oral alternative for A-06 requires documented need and
must still assess real-time reasoned response.

## Human evaluation

No external evaluator or mandatory human sign-off is required for this Year 1
course. The independent curriculum facilitator evaluates the course work under
the published standards. Optional human review may be requested for writing or
oral communication, but routine external mentoring is not required in Years
1–3. Any reviewer receives only the minimum necessary student work and no private
devotional, counseling, or pastoral information.
