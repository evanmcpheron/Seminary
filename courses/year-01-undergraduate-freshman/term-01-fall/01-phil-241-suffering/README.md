# PHIL 241 — Suffering

## Purpose

PHIL 241 introduces the philosophical and theological problem of suffering and
evil at an undergraduate level. The course begins with conceptual and biblical
orientation, develops the logical and evidential arguments from evil, compares
major Christian responses, and concludes with confessional and pastoral
synthesis. It trains the student to argue rigorously without treating suffering
as merely an abstraction or using theology to silence lament.

## Prerequisites

None. The verified 2026–27 catalog entry states no prerequisite or corequisite.
Because PHIL 101 is concurrent in the reconciled curriculum, early weeks include
explicit scaffolding in argument reconstruction and evaluation.

## Learning outcomes

See [learning-outcomes.md](learning-outcomes.md). Seven measurable outcomes cover
conceptual analysis, argument reconstruction, comparative evaluation, biblical
interpretation, charitable representation, confessional synthesis, and pastoral
discernment.

## Required materials

The designed course requires C. S. Lewis's *The Problem of Pain*, Michael L.
Peterson's *The Problem of Evil: Selected Readings*, the ESV and NASB 2020, three
free reference entries, and one free comparative media item. See
[bibliography.md](bibliography.md) for adopted designations and the verified
source records under `resources/source-records/` for access details.

## Assessment

Seven graded components total 100 percent and map to every learning outcome:
reading and argument journals, retrieval quizzes, an argument reconstruction
brief, a midterm examination, a comparative analysis paper, an oral defense
dialogue, and a cumulative final integrative examination. See
[assessment-map.md](assessment-map.md).

## Workload and term pattern

This four-credit course requires exactly 180 hours under the project's
45-hours-per-credit model. Weeks 1–14 each require 12 hours and include two
substantial written lectures. Week 15 requires 12 hours for final synthesis and
the final assessment, with no new instructional content. See
[schedule.md](schedule.md).
