# Assessment and Assignment Map

This document fixes the course-level assessment architecture. It does not contain
student-facing prompts, exam questions, rubrics, answer keys, or sample responses.
Those materials may be generated only under their later prompts, after the
relevant lectures and readings have been approved.

## Graded structure

| ID | Assessment | Design specification | Mode | Weight | Student work |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| A-01 | Reading and argument journals | Twelve concise entries that identify a source's claim, reasons, objection, and significance; assigned in weeks 1–6 and 8–13 | Open-book written | 10% | 6 hours |
| A-02 | Retrieval quizzes | Six cumulative terminology and argument-structure checks in weeks 2, 4, 6, 9, 11, and 13 | Closed-book, timed | 10% | 3 hours |
| A-03 | Argument reconstruction brief | One short reconstruction and preliminary evaluation of a major argument from evil | Open-book written | 10% | 5 hours |
| A-04 | Midterm examination | Cumulative assessment of concepts, biblical foundations, logical argument, and major responses through week 7 | Closed-book, timed written | 15% | 7 hours |
| A-05 | Comparative analysis paper | One focused comparison of an argument from evil and two materially different responses, including a strong counterargument | Take-home written | 20% | 10 hours |
| A-06 | Oral defense dialogue | One recorded defense of a constructive position followed by responses to objections | Recorded oral | 10% | 5 hours |
| A-07 | Final integrative examination | Cumulative synthesis of philosophical, biblical-theological, comparative, confessional, and pastoral reasoning | Time-limited take-home written | 25% | 18 hours |
|  | **Total** |  |  | **100%** | **54 hours** |

The final examination's 18 hours comprise 6 hours of cumulative preparation in
weeks 12–14 and 12 hours in the final-assessment week. The other assessments
account for the remaining 36 assessment hours.

## Scoring architecture to be implemented later

- Each graded item must receive a student-facing rubric or scoring standard.
- The midterm, quizzes, oral follow-up questions, and final examination require
  separate instructor materials and may not expose answer standards in the
  student-facing files.
- Argumentative work will be scored for accurate representation, reconstruction,
  evidence, objection handling, and clarity. Confessional agreement alone does
  not earn credit, and disagreement must not be penalized when the stated task
  and evidence standards are met.
- Biblical work will be scored for contextual interpretation and responsible use
  of the required translations, not proof-text accumulation.
- Pastoral judgment will be scored for attentive listening, non-coercive language,
  appropriate limits, and recognition of when referral or immediate safety action
  is warranted; it will not simulate clinical licensure.

## Authorship, sequencing, and retake controls

`A-03`, `A-05`, `A-06`, and `A-07` require an AI-use declaration. Substantive
student prose and oral answers must be the student's own. Later production must
generate and source-audit lectures before creating any dependent graded task.

The undergraduate progression threshold is a final course grade of C (73%). One
structured retake may be offered after documented remediation. The original
result, remediation, retake result, and final progression decision must all be
retained.
