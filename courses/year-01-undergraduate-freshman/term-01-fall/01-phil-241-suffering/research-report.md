## Research map for later design

The verified source set is broad enough for Prompt 05 to make deliberate choices
within these domains:

- conceptual distinctions among pain, suffering, moral evil, natural evil, and
  the existential/religious dimensions of evil;
- logical and evidential arguments from evil, including Mackie, Rowe, and Draper;
- defenses, theodicies, and anti-theodicy cautions, including Plantinga, Hick,
  Adams, Stump, and skeptical theism;
- Christian philosophical and theological evaluation grounded in Scripture and
  attentive to creation, fall, providence, lament, incarnation, cross,
  resurrection, judgment, and new creation;
- historical sources available through a verified anthology, including Job,
  Augustine, Aquinas, Leibniz, and Hume;
- rival and internal criticisms presented in their strongest responsible form;
- comparative non-Christian perspectives without treating distinct traditions
  as interchangeable; and
- the difference between analyzing an argument and responding pastorally to a
  sufferer. Later content should never use abstract argument to dismiss trauma,
  grief, abuse, or lament.

This map is research coverage, not a course outline or sequence.

## Argument and perspective coverage

The source inventory supports accurate and charitable treatment of these major
positions:

- Mackie's logical argument and Plantinga's free-will defense;
- Rowe's evidential argument, Draper's probabilistic development, and Wykstra's
  skeptical-theist reply;
- Hick's soul-making approach, Adams's account of horrendous evils, and Stump's
  narrative/second-personal approach;
- philosophical and theological differences between a defense and a theodicy;
- evangelical theological-system comparison through Feinberg and a
  biblical-theological complement through Wright; and
- Eastern philosophical and religious accounts of suffering through a
  transcript-supported comparative media source.

The Peterson anthology's publisher-verified contributor list supplies direct
opposing positions as well as replies. Later confessional evaluation must follow,
not replace, fair exposition of those positions.

## Comparable academic models

Two models were consulted for research architecture only:

1. Keith DeRose's Yale philosophy-of-religion reading pages pair Plantinga with
   Mackie and structure an evidential-evil exchange around Rowe, Wykstra, Hick,
   and Adams. This supports dialogical source pairing, not copying a syllabus.
2. The University of Notre Dame Press anthology groups historical sources,
   atheistic arguments, defenses, theodicies, and recent debates. Its verified
   breadth supports using a single anthology to reduce student cost.
