# Week 5 Objectives — Free Will and Moral Evil

By the end of this week, the student will be able to:

1. distinguish a defense from a theodicy by their aims, burdens, and warranted
   conclusions (`LO-01`, `LO-03`);
2. distinguish a logically possible world from a world feasible for God to
   actualize while preserving significantly free creaturely action (`LO-01`,
   `LO-02`);
3. reconstruct Plantinga's free-will defense as a reply to Mackie's claim that
   God could create free persons who always freely choose rightly (`LO-02`,
   `LO-03`);
4. explain morally significant freedom, actualization, and transworld
   depravity without claiming that freedom logically entails sin or that
   transworld depravity is actual or necessary (`LO-01`, `LO-02`);
5. identify the limited modal conclusion supported if Plantinga's possibility
   premise succeeds (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
6. compare Plantinga's defense with Lewis's more ambitious account of human
   pain and evaluate the additional truth, moral, and pastoral burdens Lewis's
   proposal assumes (`LO-03`, `LO-06`);
7. test the free-will defense against objections concerning libertarian
   freedom, feasible worlds, selective intervention, heaven, natural evil,
   proportionality, and divine providence (`LO-02`, `LO-03`, `LO-06`); and
8. formulate a provisional confessional judgment that affirms responsible
   creaturely agency while refusing to excuse perpetrators, blame sufferers,
   infer a case-specific divine reason, or treat logical consistency as
   pastoral care (`LO-03`, `LO-06`).

These objectives concern argument analysis and constructive synthesis. They do
not require personal disclosure, authorize speculation about a particular
tragedy, or imply that preventing abuse and pursuing justice would improperly
interfere with another person's freedom.
