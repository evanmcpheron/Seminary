# Instructor Facilitation Guide — Week 5 Modal Hearing

## Purpose and preparation

The exercise tests accurate representation, modal reasoning, charitable
objection, and scope control. It does not assess confessional assent. Have the
Plantinga and Lewis assignments, both audited lectures, and the Week 5
source-audit available.

## Facilitation sequence

1. Require each participant to distinguish defense, theodicy, pastoral
   response, and scope error before advocacy begins.
2. For Hearing 1, record statements under **possible condition**, **asserted
   actual reason**, **pastoral action**, or **unsupported inference**.
3. For Hearing 2, display three columns: **logically possible**, **feasible for
   God**, and **not established**.
4. Require Plantinga's advocate to preserve libertarian freedom throughout the
   argument and to use possibility language for transworld depravity.
5. Require Mackie's advocate to direct the objection at a premise, definition,
   or modal inference rather than at the advocate's motives.
6. Reserve the final five minutes for the limited logical verdict, one
   unresolved question, and the pastoral boundary.

## Source-controlled anchors

- A defense can answer a strict inconsistency allegation through a relevant
  coherent possibility; it does not thereby identify God's actual reason.
- The possibility of a world where free creatures always do right does not by
  itself show that God can actualize that world while preserving the same free
  responses.
- Transworld depravity is introduced as a possible feasibility condition, not
  as an actual or necessary diagnosis of every person.
- Plantinga's argument concerns moral evil most directly. Natural evil,
  selective intervention, heaven, and the distribution of harm remain live
  pressures.
- Lewis's stronger claims about formation and providence should be identified
  as such and tested rather than folded into Plantinga's limited conclusion.

## Strong objection range

Accept compatibilist freedom, counterfactual-grounding, modal-warrant,
selective-intervention, heavenly-freedom, natural-evil, or disproportion
objections when the participant identifies the target and resulting scope.
Participants may concede or narrow the defense. Do not require them to pretend
that every objection has a decisive answer.

## Corrections to make promptly

- “Possible means God can actualize it.” Ask what creaturely counterfactuals
  and undetermined responses add to feasibility.
- “Transworld depravity means everyone necessarily sins.” Require the
  possibility/feasibility formulation.
- “The defense explains why this atrocity occurred.” Ask when the argument
  crossed from a defense to an actual theodicy.
- “Providential good makes the wrong act good.” Require separation of God's
  ordering from the wrongdoer's intention and responsibility.
- “A logical answer resolves the victim's present need.” Return to safety,
  lament, presence, and practical help.

## Pastoral and integrity boundaries

Use only the fictional case. Do not solicit personal disclosure or private
third-party stories. If a participant discloses danger, abuse, or risk of harm,
stop the exercise and follow the course safety and referral policy. Do not
reward agreement with the course's confessional conclusion; evaluate faithful
representation and reasoning.

## Completion evidence

A completed discussion includes two role-based hearings, two distinct
objections, a five-sentence joint verdict, and one private scope revision per
participant. The work remains ungraded guided practice.
