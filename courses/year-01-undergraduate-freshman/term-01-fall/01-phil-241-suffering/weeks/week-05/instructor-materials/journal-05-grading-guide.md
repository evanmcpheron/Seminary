# Instructor Grading Guide — A-01-J05

Use the public 10-point rubric in `../rubric.md`. This guide clarifies source,
scope, and authorship standards; it does not provide a model journal entry.

## Threshold checks

Confirm that the response:

- is 300–400 words, excluding metadata and the source entry;
- uses all five required headings;
- selects one focused Plantinga or Lewis claim;
- provides a locatable assigned-edition page or chapter location;
- includes a source entry and AI-use declaration; and
- avoids asserting an actual divine reason for a particular person's
  suffering.

Minor length or documentation defects ordinarily affect the final criterion.
Fabricated evidence or prohibited AI authorship is an integrity matter rather
than an ordinary deduction.

## Content guidance

A high-quality Plantinga entry will distinguish the logical work of a defense
from the explanatory burden of a theodicy. If it uses possible worlds,
feasibility, or transworld depravity, it should preserve possibility language
and avoid treating a possible all-right world as automatically feasible.

A high-quality Lewis entry will state his stronger formative or providential
claim accurately, distinguish God's ordering from the wrongdoer's intention,
and test the claim against a serious moral, evidential, theological, or
pastoral objection. It should not represent Lewis's proposal as Plantinga's
logical conclusion.

The strongest objections target a premise, definition, inference, evidential
burden, moral implication, theological commitment, or pastoral use. A generic
statement that suffering is difficult does not yet satisfy this criterion.

## Scoring cautions

- Do not require agreement with Plantinga, Lewis, or the course's confessional
  judgment.
- Do not treat chapter titles, lecture reconstructions, or student evaluations
  as direct source quotations.
- Do not reward a confident case-specific explanation unsupported by the
  source and evidence.
- Do not penalize a student for concluding that the selected argument succeeds
  only in part.
- Do not infer AI use from polish alone; evaluate the declaration and available
  process evidence under the course policy.

## Feedback boundary

Identify the criterion with questions such as “What does this possibility
establish?” or “Which premise does the objection pressure?” Do not select the
student's source claim, supply the two reasons, formulate the objection, or
rewrite the judgment.
