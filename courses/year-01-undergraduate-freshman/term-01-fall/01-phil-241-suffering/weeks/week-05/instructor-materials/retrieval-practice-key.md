# Instructor Key — Week 5 Retrieval Practice

Keep this file separate from the student practice. Use it for self-correction,
not grading. Accept philosophically equivalent wording that preserves the
scope distinctions.

## Part I — Distinctions

1. A **defense** proposes a relevant possible condition that defeats an
   objection without claiming to state God's actual reason. A **theodicy**
   offers an account of God's actual reasons or purposes.
2. A person has **morally significant freedom** when the person is free with
   respect to an action that is morally right or wrong; on Plantinga's model,
   both doing and refraining must be open in the relevant sense.
3. A **possible world** is a complete way reality could be without
   contradiction. A **feasible world** is one God can actualize given the free
   creaturely responses that would occur in its relevant circumstances.
4. Causal determination would settle the choice, while libertarian freedom
   requires the relevant choice not to be causally settled by another agent;
   the two descriptions conflict in the same respect.
5. A counterfactual of creaturely freedom states what a creature would freely
   do if placed in specified circumstances.
6. Transworld depravity is the possible condition in which a creatable
   significantly free person would go wrong at least once in every world God
   could actualize containing that person's significant freedom. It is not the
   claim that the person necessarily sins in every possible world.
7. No. Plantinga can grant that an always-right free world is possible while
   denying that its possibility makes it feasible for God to actualize.
8. **Simple evil** is the creature's rebellious willing or action considered
   as evil. **Complex good** is God's good ordering or redemptive use of a
   history that includes such evil without making the evil intention good.

## Part II — Scope diagnoses

9. **Accurate element:** transworld depravity belongs to Plantinga's response
   to the always-right-world objection. **Correction:** the defense needs the
   condition to be possible; it does not prove it actual of every actual
   person.
10. **Accurate element:** an always-right free world may be possible.
    **Correction:** possibility alone does not entail feasibility, because God
    does not causally determine the creaturely response while preserving it as
    libertarian-free in the same respect.
11. **Accurate element:** significant freedom may provide a possible reason for
    permitting some moral evil. **Correction:** this does not directly explain
    earthquakes, animal suffering, or natural evil generally.
12. **Accurate element:** providence may bring good from a wrongful act.
    **Correction:** the wrongdoer's evil intention remains blameworthy, and the
    later good does not excuse the act.

A successful final sentence will say that a **defense** may use a **possible**
condition to show why an all-right free world need not be **feasible**, while a
**theodicy** bears the further burden of identifying an actual explanation.
