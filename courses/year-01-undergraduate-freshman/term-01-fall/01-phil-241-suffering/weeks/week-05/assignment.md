## A-01-J05 — Reading and Argument Journal 5

**Parent assessment:** A-01 Reading and argument journals

**Status:** graded, 10 points within the twelve-entry A-01 series

**Time budget:** 0.50 hour

**Outcomes:** `LO-01`, `LO-03`, `LO-06`

### Purpose

Represent one Week 5 source claim accurately, classify its argumentative
burden, and test it with a strong objection without inflating a defense into a
theodicy.

### Task

Choose **one** focused claim from Plantinga's assigned chapter or Lewis
chapters 6–7. Write **300–400 words** under five headings:

1. **Focused source claim:** State the claim precisely, identify its author,
   and give the assigned-edition page or chapter location.
2. **Argumentative status:** Classify it as part of a defense, a theodicy, or a
   more limited supporting claim. Explain the classification rather than merely
   naming it.
3. **Reasons and role:** Reconstruct at least two reasons or distinctions that
   support the claim and state what work it does in the author's argument.
4. **Strong objection:** Present the strongest relevant objection. Identify
   whether it challenges logical possibility, freedom, feasibility, evidence,
   moral adequacy, theological coherence, or pastoral use.
5. **Provisional judgment and scope:** State what the claim explains or
   establishes, what it does not establish, and one qualification required for
   a responsible Christian use.

Use paraphrase. A direct quotation is not required. If quoting, keep the
quotation brief and cite the exact location in the lawful copy used. Do not
assert that transworld depravity is actual or necessary, infer God's reason for
a particular person's suffering, excuse a wrongdoer, or require personal
disclosure.

## A-04-P01 — Midterm Preparation 1

**Parent assessment:** A-04 Midterm examination

**Status:** required preparation milestone; ungraded and not itself an exam

**Time budget:** 1.50 hours of the approved Week 5 A-04 preparation allocation

**Outcomes prepared:** `LO-01`, `LO-02`, `LO-03`

This milestone begins cumulative retrieval for the Week 7 closed-book timed
midterm. It does not contain, predict, or replace the later exam questions.

### Time-boxed process

| Step | Deliverable | Time |
|---|---|---:|
| Build the coverage grid | List Weeks 1–5 topics, source voices, and linked outcomes from memory; then check course materials | 0.25 h |
| Closed-book definitions | Define twelve central terms from memory, including four Week 5 terms | 0.25 h |
| Reconstruct arguments | From memory, write one historical or skeptical argument and one logical/free-will exchange in numbered premise-conclusion form | 0.33 h |
| Source and scope check | Use assigned materials to correct attribution, missing premises, and overclaimed conclusions in a different color or correction column | 0.33 h |
| Objection pairing | Pair each map with one strong objection and identify its exact premise or inference | 0.17 h |
| Remediation plan | Identify the three weakest retrieval areas and schedule specific review actions before Week 6 preparation | 0.17 h |
|  | **Total** | **1.50 h** |

### Required preparation record

Submit or retain one concise document containing:

1. a Weeks 1–5 coverage grid;
2. twelve closed-book definitions and visible corrections;
3. two numbered argument maps, each with source attribution;
4. one strong objection paired with each map;
5. a list of at least three corrected scope or attribution errors; and
6. a three-item remediation plan with dates or study blocks.

At least four definitions must come from Week 5: *defense*, *theodicy*,
*feasible world*, and *transworld depravity*. One argument map must distinguish
Mackie's always-right-world objection from Plantinga's response. The second may
come from Augustine, Aquinas, Leibniz, Hume/Philo, Mackie's logical argument,
or another source already assigned in Weeks 1–4.

### AI and integrity control

Do the closed-book retrieval and first argument maps without sources, web, AI,
or assistance. During the correction phase, use the assigned sources and
audited lectures, not generated answers. AI may explain a named concept only
after the correction record is complete; it may not produce or repair the
maps, objections, or remediation plan. Record any permitted assistance. See
the completion standard in [rubric.md](rubric.md).
