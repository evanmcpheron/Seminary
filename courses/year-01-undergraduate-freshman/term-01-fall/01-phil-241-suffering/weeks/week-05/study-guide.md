## 3. World and feasibility map

Use these fictional world descriptions:

- `W-A`: persons are causally determined always to do right and never do wrong;
- `W-B`: persons are significantly free and always freely do right;
- `W-C`: persons are significantly free and at least one freely does wrong;
- `W-D`: persons are described as both causally determined by God to do right
  and undetermined by God in that same choice and respect.

For each world, answer:

1. Is the description logically possible on Plantinga's definitions?
2. If possible, is it automatically feasible for God to actualize? Why or why
   not?
3. Does the world contain significant moral good, moral evil, both, or neither?
4. Which world best exposes the difference between *possible* and *feasible*?

Now reconstruct the critical exchange in eight numbered lines or fewer:

- Mackie's improved-world objection;
- Plantinga's distinction between possibility and feasibility;
- the possible role of universal transworld depravity; and
- the limited conclusion.

Mark every line that is your reconstruction rather than a sentence from the
source.

## 4. Transworld-depravity scope test

Mark each statement **required by the defense**, **consistent but not
required**, or **misstatement**.

1. It is possible that every candidate significantly free creature would go
   wrong in at least one relevant feasible circumstance.
2. Every possible world with free creatures contains moral evil.
3. A free creature who never sins is a contradiction.
4. Universal transworld depravity is actual.
5. Universal transworld depravity is necessary.
6. A possible all-right free world need not be feasible for God to actualize.
7. God can causally determine an action and leave it libertarian-free in the
   same respect.
8. If the possibility premise is coherent, the strict inconsistency claim has
   a countermodel.

Rewrite each misstatement so that it expresses a claim Plantinga's defense can
use.

## 5. Objection classification

For each objection, identify its most direct target: **logical possibility**,
**theory of freedom**, **feasibility machinery**, **actual theodicy**,
**evidential probability**, **theological coherence**, or **pastoral use**.
Some may receive two labels if you explain the relation.

1. Compatibilism can preserve responsibility without categorical alternative
   possibilities.
2. Counterfactuals about never-actualized free choices lack a truth-maker.
3. God might prevent a weapon from functioning without determining the
   assailant's intention.
4. Redeemed persons in the new creation do not sin yet remain genuinely free.
5. Human freedom does not directly explain an earthquake or animal suffering.
6. This world's quantity and distribution of harm seem disproportionate to
   the good of freedom.
7. A person uses the defense to tell a victim not to report abuse.

Choose one objection. State the exact premise it attacks, one plausible reply,
and whether the reply answers the objection fully or only partly.
