## Reading questions

1. How does Plantinga distinguish the task of a defense from the task of a
   theodicy?
2. What makes freedom *morally significant* in his argument?
3. Why does a possible world in which free creatures always do right not by
   itself show that God can determine or actualize that world while preserving
   those choices as free?
4. What is the difference between logical possibility and feasibility for
   divine actualization?
5. What work does the possibility of transworld depravity do? What does the
   defense not require us to claim about its actuality or necessity?
6. Does Plantinga argue that free will logically entails moral evil? Explain.
7. Which objections address the strict consistency model, and which instead
   address an actual theodicy, the probability of theism, or pastoral use?
8. Which claims in Lewis's chapters propose what suffering actually does under
   providence rather than merely what it possibly could do?
9. How does Lewis's denial that suffering is good in itself constrain appeals
   to character, surrender, compassion, or complex good?
10. What would be wrong with using either author to tell a sufferer, without
    evidence, why a particular evil occurred?
