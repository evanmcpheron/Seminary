# Lecture 2 Outline — Plantinga's Free-Will Defense and Its Limits

## Objectives

- Reconstruct Mackie's always-right-free-creatures objection and Plantinga's
  reply.
- Distinguish a possible world from a world feasible for God to actualize.
- Explain significant freedom and divine actualization without confusing
  nondetermination with randomness or moral approval.
- State the role of transworld depravity with the correct modal scope.
- Evaluate objections concerning freedom, feasibility, intervention, heaven,
  natural evil, amount, and providence.
- State exactly what the defense establishes if successful and what it leaves
  open.

## Outline

1. The target: Mackie's improved-world objection
2. Significant freedom and morally significant action
3. Possible worlds and creaturely counterfactuals
4. Actualization, causal determination, and feasibility
5. Why an always-right free world is not enough
6. Transworld depravity as a possibility premise
7. A premise-conclusion reconstruction
8. Objections from freedom and feasibility
9. Selective intervention, heaven, and divine freedom
10. Natural evil, amount, and distribution
11. Providence, culpability, and confessional evaluation
12. A precise verdict and transition to Week 6

## Key terms

Significant freedom; libertarian freedom; compatibilism; moral good; moral
evil; possible world; feasible world; actualization; strong actualization;
counterfactual of creaturely freedom; creaturely essence; transworld
depravity; universal transworld depravity; selective intervention; defense;
