# Week 5 Guided Discussion — The Modal Hearing

This is **ungraded guided practice**. Work with one partner when available. If
working alone, record both sides and pause between roles. Use only the
fictional cases below; no personal history or private third-party story may be
required or recorded.

## Roles and time

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Individual preparation | 10 min |
| Hearing 1: defense or theodicy? | 15 min |
| Hearing 2: possible or feasible? | 20 min |
| Objection panel | 10 min |
| Joint verdict and revision | 5 min |
| **Total** | **60 min** |

Partner A begins as **Mackie's advocate** and Partner B as **Plantinga's
advocate**. Switch roles after Hearing 1. The goal is accurate representation,
not winning.

## Preparation

Each participant prepares:

- a two-sentence distinction between defense and theodicy;
- a four-line map of Mackie's always-right-world objection;
- a four-line map of Plantinga's feasibility reply;
- a scope-controlled definition of transworld depravity; and
- one objection that targets the defense and one that instead targets a fuller
  theodicy.

## Hearing 1 — Defense or theodicy?

Classify each statement as a defense claim, theodicy claim, pastoral response,
or scope error. Give the strongest charitable reason for the classification.

1. “Possibly, the value of significant freedom gives God a morally sufficient
   reason to permit some moral evil.”
2. “The freedom defense proves why every actual atrocity was permitted.”
3. “Lewis proposes that God can order a history containing creaturely evil
   toward redemptive good without making the evil intention good.”
4. “Because God can bring good from evil, a bystander need not intervene.”
5. “The logical argument and a report of fresh harm require different kinds of
   response.”

The advocate of the classified position must first restate it in the strongest
defensible form. The other participant then names the exact extra premise or
evidence needed for a larger conclusion.

## Hearing 2 — Possible or feasible?

Consider the fictional person **Mara** and action **R**:

- it is possible that Mara is significantly free regarding R and freely does
  what is right;
- it is possible that Mara is significantly free regarding R and freely does
  what is wrong;
- God can choose whether to create Mara and place her in circumstance C;
- on Plantinga's model, God does not causally determine Mara's significantly
  free response in C.

Mackie's advocate argues:

> Since there is a possible world in which Mara freely does right, an
> omnipotent God can actualize that world.

Plantinga's advocate must:

1. distinguish possibility from feasibility;
2. explain why causal guarantee conflicts with libertarian freedom in the same
   respect;
3. introduce transworld depravity without claiming it actual or necessary; and
4. state the limited conclusion.

Mackie's advocate then raises the strongest of these objections: compatibilist
freedom, counterfactual grounding, insufficient reason to accept the
possibility premise, or selective intervention. Plantinga's advocate may
answer, concede, or narrow the defense, but may not change the definition of
freedom midargument.

Switch roles and repeat with a different objection.

## Objection panel

Classify the following as principally attacking the logical defense or a fuller
theodicy. Explain what would follow if the objection succeeds.

- Human freedom does not directly explain natural evil.
- A world with fewer successful harms might preserve offenders' intentions and
  victims' freedom.
- Heaven appears to contain free redeemed persons without sin.
- Lewis's formation account does not fit every sufferer's outcome.
- A possible reason supplies no warrant for diagnosing a particular tragedy.

## Joint verdict

Compose five sentences together:

1. Mackie's strongest objection;
2. Plantinga's strongest reply;
3. the exact logical result if the reply succeeds;
4. the most important unresolved evidential or theological question; and
5. the pastoral boundary on using the result.

Each participant privately revises one sentence that overstated either side.
