# Week 6 Objectives — Natural Evil and Providence

By the end of this week, the student will be able to:

1. distinguish moral evil, natural evil, and mixed causal cases without using
   the classification to erase human responsibility (`LO-02`, `LO-07`);
2. reconstruct Swinburne's natural-evil proposal as an extension of a
   free-will theodicy through knowledge of consequences and opportunities for
   morally significant choice (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
3. explain Stump's objections that actual natural evil is neither shown to be
   necessary nor shown to be a better means of acquiring the relevant
   knowledge (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
4. analyze animal suffering as pressure on human-centered accounts and
   evaluate Lewis's speculative proposals without treating them as
   established explanations (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
5. distinguish stable-order, higher-order-good, fall, nonhuman-agency,
   compensation, outweighing, and defeat proposals by their claims and burdens
   (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
6. distinguish Chisholmian defeat from merely adding enough unrelated good to
   outweigh an evil and state why neither category proves that a particular
   evil is justified (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
7. interpret Job 38–42 and Romans 8:18–39 as canonical constraints on
   providence claims, lament, creaturely limits, and hope, using the completed
   ESV and NASB 2020 comparison work responsibly (`LO-04`); and
8. formulate a pastorally responsible response that supports protection,
   relief, lament, and referral while declining to infer why a particular
   person or animal suffered (`LO-07`).

These objectives concern philosophical, biblical-theological, and pastoral
judgment. They require no personal disclosure and provide no authorization to
diagnose a sufferer, discourage medical or safety action, or identify a hidden
divine purpose in an individual case.
