# Week 6 — Natural Evil and Providence

## Weekly purpose

Week 6 asks whether responses centered on creaturely freedom can illuminate
suffering not straightforwardly caused by a culpable choice. Lecture 1
distinguishes moral, natural, and mixed cases; examines animal suffering; and
reconstructs Richard Swinburne's claim that natural evils can supply knowledge
and opportunities needed for morally significant choice. Eleonore Stump's
reply tests whether suffering is necessary or preferable as a means of gaining
that knowledge. C. S. Lewis's explicitly speculative account of animal pain is
examined without treating his claims about animal consciousness or prehuman
corruption as established fact.

Lecture 2 distinguishes outweighing, compensation, and Roderick Chisholm's
technical category of defeat. It then tests general appeals to providence,
stable order, formation, judgment, and eschatological hope. The result is
deliberately limited: a general account may identify a possible or actual kind
of divine reason without identifying God's reason for a particular loss.
Biblical providence authorizes lament, care, protection, and hope; it does not
license blame, passivity, or case-specific speculation.
