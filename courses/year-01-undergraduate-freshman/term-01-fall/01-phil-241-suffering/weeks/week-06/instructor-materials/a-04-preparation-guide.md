# Instructor Guide — A-04-P02 Midterm Preparation 2

This guide supports a required ungraded preparation milestone. It is not a
practice exam, exam key, predicted-question list, or disclosure of the Week 7
midterm.

## Completion audit

Mark the milestone complete only when the record contains:

- completion or reasoned rescheduling of all three A-04-P01 remediation items;
- eighteen definitions visibly attempted closed-book and then corrected;
- accurate definitions for the six specified Week 6 terms;
- three numbered, source-attributed argument maps first attempted closed-book;
- an eight-row source-voice matrix with claim, target, limitation, and visible
  corrections;
- a mixed-case analysis separating cause, responsibility, immediate duty, and
  all three providence-claim levels;
- at least six corrections, including attribution, structure, biblical
  context, and scope;
- a ranked three-item remediation plan with dates or study blocks; and
- an honest record of permitted assistance.

Do not attach points or fold completion into the A-04 exam score.

## Minimum accuracy anchors

Use these anchors to diagnose rather than supply replacement answers:

- *natural evil* identifies suffering not immediately classified as the result
  of culpable creaturely choice;
- a *mixed causal case* includes natural processes and morally relevant action
  or omission;
- a *stable-order account* appeals to regularity as a condition for embodied
  agency, learning, planning, and responsibility;
- a *necessary condition* must be present for the stated end to obtain;
- *defeat* is not mere compensation or outweighing; and
- a *token explanation* assigns a reason to a particular event.

Argument maps should preserve these boundaries:

- the historical/biblical map attributes speakers and reconstructed premises
  carefully;
- the Mackie–Plantinga map distinguishes possible from feasible worlds and
  limits the conclusion to consistency;
- the Swinburne–Stump map identifies a knowledge-and-choice proposal and a
  necessity/alternatives objection without claiming Stump refutes every
  natural-evil account.

The source-voice matrix must not turn dialogue speakers into simple authorial
claims, merge Mackie and Hume, attribute Plantinga's terminology to Augustine,
or present Lewis's animal-pain speculation as established.

## Diagnostic categories

Record only the category and a prompt for repair:

- **Definition:** “What limiting condition belongs in this term?”
- **Attribution:** “Which speaker or assigned chapter supports this line?”
- **Structure:** “What premise connects the good to permission?”
- **Biblical context:** “Does this passage give a token cause, or a canonical
  frame?”
- **Scope:** “Is this possible, necessary, actual, general, kind-level, or
  token?”
- **Objection fit:** “Which premise does the criticism challenge?”
- **Pastoral application:** “What action remains required under uncertainty?”
- **Retrieval process:** “Was this written before sources were opened?”

Do not give a corrected definition, map, source match, case analysis, or future
exam response.

## Remediation routing

- Send term errors to completed weekly glossaries and study guides.
- Send source confusion to the assigned chapter and reading annotation.
- Send map errors to the specific Week 3–6 reconstruction exercise already
  completed.
- Send natural-evil scope errors to Lecture 1's six tests.
- Send providence and value-relation errors to Lecture 2 and the Week 6 study
  guide.
- Require each final remediation action to name a topic, source, method,
  duration, and scheduled block before Week 7.

## Integrity boundary

The initial definitions, maps, matrix, and case classification must remain the
student's unaided retrieval. Assistance may clarify a named concept only after
the correction record is complete and may not produce or repair maps, source
matches, case analysis, remediation, or future exam answers. Preserve the
record as process evidence without converting it into a graded assessment.
