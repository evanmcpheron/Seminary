# Instructor Facilitation Guide — Week 6 Discussion

This guide supports a fictional, ungraded exercise. Do not solicit personal
trauma, infer legal liability, or let philosophical discussion delay animal
care, safety assessment, truthful investigation, or required reporting.

## Expected distinctions

The stipulated case is mixed. The storm is a natural process. The delayed
repair is a potentially culpable omission, but final culpability would require
facts about responsibility, notice, available alternatives, and causal
contribution. The injured animals bear the immediate cost; community learning
or compassion cannot by itself justify that cost.

The first volunteer erases the possible omission by treating *natural* as an
exclusive classification. The second moves from general providence or a
possible higher-order good to an unsupported token explanation and assigns a
purpose that benefits others. Neither statement removes immediate duties.

## Role-quality indicators

### Analyst A

- separates storm, structural failure, prior notice, delayed repair, and injury;
- identifies unknown evidence before assigning culpability;
- distinguishes causal classification from divine justification; and
- names care and safety duties without issuing a legal conclusion.

### Analyst B

- explains that stable regularities allow planning and responsibility;
- presents Swinburne's knowledge or opportunity proposal charitably;
- limits the result to a possible kind-level reason for some risk; and
- does not call this injury necessary, deserved, or already redeemed.

### Analyst C

- presses Stump's necessity and better-means objections at the correct premise;
- asks who bears the cost and receives the good;
- distinguishes compensation, outweighing, and defeat; and
- joins critique to practical animal care and truthful review.

## Facilitator prompts

Use prompts that expose structure without supplying a model response:

- “Which cause does that sentence classify?”
- “What additional evidence would justify culpability?”
- “Is that a general, kind-level, or token claim?”
- “Which Swinburne premise is under pressure?”
- “Would the alleged good exist independently of this harm?”
- “Good for whom?”
- “What can be done before this disagreement is resolved?”

Redirect motive attacks toward premises. If a participant uses providence to
excuse neglect, ask whether divine permission transfers moral authorization to
the responsible human agent. If a participant treats all theodicy as cruelty,
ask whether a general reason can be explored while token application remains
forbidden.

## Joint-statement completion check

The synthesis should:

- state a mixed classification provisionally;
- preserve evidence requirements;
- articulate the strongest kind-level providence proposal;
- name Stump's pressure and the beneficiary problem;
- use Job or Romans 8 contextually;
- withhold a token divine reason; and
- state concrete care, safety, investigation, and reporting duties.

Do not score theological agreement. Completion concerns charitable argument,
scope control, biblical context, and responsible action.
