## A-01-J06 — Reading and Argument Journal 6

**Parent assessment:** A-01 Reading and argument journals

**Status:** graded, 10 points within the twelve-entry A-01 series

**Time budget:** 0.50 hour

**Outcomes:** `LO-03`, `LO-04`, `LO-07`

### Purpose

Evaluate one source-controlled claim about natural evil while distinguishing a
general philosophical proposal from a case-specific explanation or pastoral
pronouncement.

### Task

Write **300–400 words** under five headings:

1. **Focused source claim:** Select one claim from Swinburne, Stump, or Lewis's
   chapter 9. State it accurately and provide the assigned chapter/page or
   stable location.
2. **Reasons and argumentative role:** Reconstruct at least two reasons or
   distinctions supporting the claim. Identify whether it functions as a
   defense, theodicy, objection, hypothesis, or admitted limitation.
3. **Strong pressure:** Present the strongest relevant objection or reply from
   another Week 6 source. Name the precise premise, inference, or scope claim
   it challenges.
4. **Providence and Scripture boundary:** Use Job 38–42 or Romans 8:18–39 to
   state one responsible confessional conclusion and one token inference the
   text does not authorize.
5. **Provisional judgment and pastoral limit:** State what the exchange
   clarifies, what remains unresolved, and one sentence that should not be
   imposed on a sufferer.

Use paraphrase. A direct quotation is not required. If quoting, keep it brief
and cite the exact location. Chisholm is optional and is not required for this
entry. Do not research a real tragedy or infer why any person or animal
suffered.

### Source reference and submission

End with a short-form source entry that makes the selected claim locatable.
Submit UTF-8 Markdown with this metadata:


The word count excludes metadata and the source entry. Use the reading and
study-guide notes so the work remains within 30 minutes.

### Evaluation and AI-use declaration

See the A-01-J06 section of [rubric.md](rubric.md). The twelve A-01 entries are
averaged and together constitute 10 percent of the course grade.

The substantive entry must be the student's own. AI may clarify instructions
or critique a genuine student draft; it may not select the claim, reconstruct
the reasons, formulate the objection, interpret the biblical passage, make the
judgment, or draft/rewrite prose for submission. State “No AI used” or describe
the permitted use and affected portion.

### Required preparation record

Retain or submit one document containing all of the following:

1. the A-04-P01 remediation status;
2. eighteen definitions first written closed-book and then visibly corrected;
3. six Week 6 terms: *natural evil*, *mixed causal case*, *stable-order
   account*, *necessary condition*, *defeat*, and *token explanation*;
4. three numbered argument maps first produced closed-book:
   - one historical or biblical-philosophical approach from Weeks 2–3;
   - Mackie's challenge and a Plantinga-style consistency reply from Weeks 4–5;
   - Swinburne's natural-evil extension and Stump's reply from Week 6;
5. an eight-row source-voice matrix that distinguishes source claim from the
   student's reconstruction and evaluation;
6. a short analysis of the fictional mixed case from
   [discussion.md](discussion.md), including an immediate care duty and the
   three levels of providence claim;
7. at least six visible corrections, including one attribution correction,
   one argument-structure correction, one biblical-context correction, and
   one scope correction; and
8. a ranked, scheduled three-item remediation plan for Week 7.

The eight source voices/texts must include Job, Augustine or Aquinas, Hume's
dialogue, Mackie, Plantinga, Swinburne, Stump, and Lewis. A source row may state
uncertainty during closed-book retrieval; the open-source phase must correct it
honestly rather than conceal the initial gap.

### AI and integrity control

mixed-case classification without books, notes, web, AI, or assistance. During
the correction phase, use only assigned sources and audited course materials.
AI may clarify a named concept after the correction record is complete; it may
not create or repair maps, match source voices, analyze the case, predict exam
questions, or write the remediation plan. Record any permitted assistance. See
the completion standard in [rubric.md](rubric.md).
