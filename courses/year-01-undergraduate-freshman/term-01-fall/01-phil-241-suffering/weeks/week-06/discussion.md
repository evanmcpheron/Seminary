# Week 6 Guided Discussion — A Mixed Case under Providence

This is **ungraded guided practice**. It may be completed with a partner or as
a recorded self-dialogue. Use only the fictional case below. Do not disclose
personal suffering, diagnose anyone, or substitute the exercise for medical,
legal, mental-health, animal-care, or safety expertise.

## Fictional case

A severe storm damages a wildlife rehabilitation center. The storm was not
caused by a human choice. Several animals are injured when part of the roof
fails. An inspection had previously identified deterioration, but the owner
postponed repair without completing a documented risk review. A volunteer says,
“The storm is natural, so no one is responsible.” Another says, “God must have
sent this exact loss to teach the community compassion.”

## Roles

- **Analyst A — causal and moral analysis:** classify the causes, identify what
  remains unknown, and state what responsibility can and cannot be inferred.
- **Analyst B — strongest providential proposal:** present the best stable-order
  or Swinburne-style account without claiming to know God's token reason.
- **Analyst C — critical reply and care boundary:** use Stump's objections,
  animal suffering, and the beneficiary question; identify immediate duties.

For a self-dialogue, give each role an uninterrupted turn before synthesizing.

## Thirty-minute protocol

### 1. Preparation — 5 minutes

Each role writes:

- one precise claim;
- one source-controlled reason;
- one distinction needed to avoid overclaiming; and
- one action appropriate before philosophical agreement is reached.

### 2. Structured exchange — 15 minutes

Address all four questions:

1. Which aspects of the case are natural, moral, or mixed? What evidence is
   still required before assigning culpability?
2. What can a stable-order or knowledge-and-choice account plausibly explain?
3. Which premise do Stump's necessity and better-means objections press?
4. Who bears the cost and who receives the alleged good? Would compensation,
   outweighing, or defeat describe the proposal accurately?

Every objection must name the premise or inference it targets. Every reply must
state one conclusion it does not establish.

### 3. Biblical and philosophical synthesis — 7 minutes

Form a joint statement of 100–150 words that:

- affirms no more than the evidence supports about causes and responsibility;
- distinguishes general providence from a token explanation;
- uses Job or Romans 8 without proof-texting or silencing lament;
- names one unresolved philosophical question; and
- lists the immediate duties of animal care, safety assessment, truthful
  investigation, and appropriate reporting.

### 4. Boundary check — 3 minutes

Identify why each opening quotation fails. Rewrite each in a form that preserves
truth, responsibility, providential humility, and practical care.

## Completion standard

The exercise is complete when all roles are represented charitably, both
source-based objections are applied to a specific premise, the joint statement
distinguishes general from token claims, and practical duties are named. The
exercise carries no points and requires no personal disclosure.
