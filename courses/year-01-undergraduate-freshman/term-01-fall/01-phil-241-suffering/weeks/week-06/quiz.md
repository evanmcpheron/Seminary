# A-02-Q03 — Retrieval Quiz 3

This is a **graded, cumulative, closed-book quiz** covering Weeks 1–6. Complete
it after all other Week 6 work.

## Conditions

- **Time:** 30 minutes total
- **Materials:** closed book, closed notes, no web, no AI, no assistance
- **Format:** eight multiple-choice items and two short analysis items
- **Points:** 20 total
- **Submission:** submit the answers and integrity statement when time ends

Spend about 10 minutes on Part I, 17 minutes on Part II, and 3 minutes checking
completion and signing the statement. Do not consult the instructor key.

## Part I — Concepts and distinctions, 8 points

Select the best answer. Each item is worth 1 point.

1. Which case is best classified as mixed?

   A. A person intentionally strikes another person.
   B. A storm causes damage after a responsible agent knowingly neglects a
   required safety repair.
   C. A harmless natural process occurs with no sentient effect.
   D. A person imagines an impossible world.

2. Swinburne's natural-evil proposal most directly connects suffering to:

   A. proof that every sufferer is guilty;
   B. knowledge of consequences and morally significant opportunities;
   C. denial that nature has regular patterns;
   D. the claim that animals possess libertarian freedom.

3. Stump's necessity objection presses which inference?

   A. From “natural evil can provide knowledge” to “no less costly route can
   provide the relevant knowledge.”
   B. From “evil exists” to “some suffering is painful.”
   C. From “Plantinga wrote a defense” to “Mackie wrote an objection.”
   D. From “Job laments” to “Job is a biblical book.”

4. Which statement best describes Chisholmian defeat?

   A. An unrelated reward numerically exceeds the harm.
   B. The sufferer forgets that the harm occurred.
   C. An evil is organically integrated into a valuable whole rather than
   merely offset by an independent good.
   D. Every evil is actually an illusion.

5. Which is a token explanation?

   A. God governs creation wisely.
   B. Stable order can support responsible agency.
   C. Creation groans while awaiting liberation.
   D. This particular loss occurred so that this observer would learn courage.

6. Lewis's chapter on animal pain should be read as:

   A. a settled scientific account of animal consciousness;
   B. an explicitly speculative exploration with admitted limits;
   C. proof that animals do not suffer;
   D. a denial of human duties toward animals.

7. Which Week 5 result remains accurate in Week 6?

   A. A possible consistency model automatically identifies God's actual
   reason.
   B. If the strict logical argument fails, every evidential question fails.
   C. A defense can answer a consistency charge while leaving actual
   explanation open.
   D. Free will logically entails moral evil.

8. Which statement best represents Job 38–42 in the Week 6 synthesis?

   A. It gives Job the friends' simple guilt explanation.
   B. It denies divine governance of nonhuman creation.
   C. It affirms divine wisdom and rule without giving the friends authority
   to infer Job's case-specific guilt.
   D. It forbids lament after suffering.

## Part II — Short analysis, 12 points

Answer each in no more than 140 words. Each item is worth 6 points.

9. Reconstruct Swinburne's move from natural regularities to morally
   significant choice in three or four lines. Then state Stump's strongest
   necessity objection and identify exactly which line it challenges. End with
   one conclusion the exchange does **not** warrant.

10. A student writes, “Romans 8 teaches that God governs creation and will
    redeem it. Therefore this animal's injury was necessary to improve the
    caretaker, and the injury has already been defeated.” Identify **three
    distinct scope or category errors**. Your answer must use the terms *token
    explanation* and *defeat* accurately and state one responsible action that
    does not wait for the philosophical dispute to be settled.
