# Week 3 Retrieval Practice — Historical Approaches

This is **ungraded practice**, not an A-02 quiz. Work without sources for 20
minutes. Then use the readings and lectures for 10 minutes to correct the work
in a different color or under a **Correction** label. Do not consult the
instructor key until both phases are complete.

## Part I — Retrieve and distinguish

Answer in one or two sentences each.

1. Define *privation* and explain why calling evil a privation does not make
   injury or suffering unreal.
2. Distinguish a direct cause from an accidental cause in Aquinas's analysis.
3. Distinguish the defect in a sinful act from the act's positive existence.
4. Distinguish fault from penalty.
5. Define *possible world* and *compossibility* in Leibniz's argument.
6. Why does Leibniz compare complete worlds rather than isolated events?
7. Name the three principal speakers in the assigned portions of Hume's
   *Dialogues*.
8. What is the difference between showing that divine goodness is compatible
   with the world and inferring divine goodness from the world?

## Part II — Reconstruct and attribute

9. In three or four numbered lines, reconstruct Augustine's reasoning from the
   goodness of natures to the conclusion that evil is corruption rather than an
   independent substance.
10. In four or five numbered lines, reconstruct the objection and the premise
    Leibniz denies in his best-world reply.
11. List Philo's four circumstances associated with misery. Then state what
    evidential conclusion he presses from the world's mixed phenomena.
12. Label each statement **Augustine/Aquinas**, **Leibniz**, **Philo**, or
    **Lewis**, and justify one label with a source location:

    a. A detailed narrative of primeval human development can be speculative
    even when the doctrinal function of the fall is affirmed.

    b. A complete world may have goods that could not be retained with every
    local evil removed.

    c. Observed effects do not warrant attributing more perfection to their
    cause than the effects support.

    d. Evil depends upon and damages a good nature.
