# Week 3 Objectives — Historical Approaches

By the end of this week, the student will be able to:

1. distinguish an ontological account of what evil is from a causal account of
   where evil comes from and an evidential argument about what evil supports
   (`LO-01`);
2. explain Augustine's account of created goodness, privation, corrupt will,
   and divine ordering without treating privation as a denial that harms are
   severe (`LO-01`, `LO-03`);
3. reconstruct Aquinas's distinctions among causing directly, causing by
   accident, permitting, fault, and penalty, then identify a serious objection
   concerning divine permission (`LO-01`, `LO-03`);
4. reconstruct Leibniz's best-world reply and identify the work done by possible
   worlds, whole-world comparison, creaturely contingency, and permission
   (`LO-01`, `LO-03`);
5. reconstruct Philo's argument in Hume's *Dialogues* from mixed phenomena to
   limits on an empirical inference to perfect goodness, while distinguishing a
   character's speech from uncomplicated authorial assertion (`LO-01`, `LO-05`);
6. compare the historical accounts with the Week 2 canonical grammar of good
   creation, creaturely rebellion, lament, providence, judgment, and hope
   (`LO-03`, `LO-04`); and
7. state the strongest pressure each historical position places on another
   before giving a provisional confessional evaluation (`LO-03`, `LO-05`).

These objectives concern arguments and texts. They do not authorize identifying
God's reason for a particular person's suffering or treating suffering as proof
of that person's guilt.
