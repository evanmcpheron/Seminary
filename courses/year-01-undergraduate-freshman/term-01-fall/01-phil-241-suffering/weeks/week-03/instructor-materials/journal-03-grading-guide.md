# Instructor Grading Guide — A-01-J03

Use the public 10-point rubric in `../rubric.md`. This guide clarifies evidence
standards; it does not supply a model student response.

## Threshold checks

Before assigning criterion scores, confirm that the response:

- selects one of the four assigned historical selections;
- falls within 300–400 words, excluding metadata and source entries;
- uses all five required headings;
- supplies locatable historical and biblical references; and
- includes an AI-use declaration.

A minor word-count or metadata defect ordinarily affects the final criterion.
Fabricated evidence or concealed prohibited AI authorship is an integrity issue,
not merely a citation deduction.

## Content guidance

High-quality work need not endorse the confessional evaluation. It must:

- distinguish the source's exact question from adjacent questions;
- reconstruct reasons rather than merely name the position;
- identify Philo, Cleanthes, or Demea when the dialogue is selected;
- pressure a real premise or inference rather than attack a person;
- use a Week 2 passage in context and label a later theological inference; and
- say both what the proposal illuminates and what it leaves open.

Do not require the student to discuss all four thinkers. Do not deduct for
rejecting Augustine, Aquinas, Leibniz, Hume, or the course's provisional
confessional judgment when representation and reasoning meet the criteria.

## Common scoring distinctions

- A summary without two supporting reasons cannot earn full credit for the
  first criterion.
- “Hume says” without speaker control is an attribution defect; the degree
  depends on whether it changes the argument.
- A privation objection that assumes “privation” means “imaginary” has not yet
  reached the strongest version of the position.
- A biblical citation used only as a slogan does not meet the canonical
  comparison standard.
- A provisional judgment that admits an unresolved question is stronger, not
  weaker, when the limit is accurately located.

## Feedback boundary

Identify the student's source or reasoning problem and ask a targeted revision
question. Do not supply replacement journal prose, construct the objection for
the student, or rewrite the canonical comparison.
