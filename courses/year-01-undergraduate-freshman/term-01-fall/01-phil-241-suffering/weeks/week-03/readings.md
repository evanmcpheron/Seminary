# Week 3 Required Reading and Source Study

Use the approved student-owned print or personally licensed digital editions
of Michael L. Peterson's anthology and C. S. Lewis's *The Problem of Pain*.
The Peterson assignment uses the verified chapter numbers and titles in the
second edition. Page numbers are not required because print and licensed
digital locations can differ. The student should record the location system
used by the acquired copy.

The anthology orders Aquinas, Leibniz, and Hume before its later Augustinian
theodicy section. Study the sources in the historical order below so that the
development from Augustine through Hume remains visible.

## Reading questions

1. What does Augustine mean by calling evil a corruption or privation of a
   good nature? Which problem does that answer, and which problems remain?
2. How can Aquinas say both that good can cause evil by accident and that God is
   not the cause of the defect in a sinful act?
3. Which premise in the objection does Leibniz deny, and what must be true of
   possible worlds for his denial to succeed?
4. Why does Philo distinguish showing that perfect goodness is compatible with
   the world from inferring perfect goodness from the world?
5. How does the dialogue form limit a simple sentence beginning “Hume says”?
6. Which parts of Lewis's fall account are presented as Christian doctrine,
   which as philosophical explanation, and which as imaginative speculation?
7. Where do the Week 2 biblical themes support, qualify, or leave open the
   historical accounts?
