# Lecture 1 Outline — Augustine and Aquinas on the Nature and Cause of Evil

## Objectives

- Distinguish ontology, causation, moral responsibility, and providence.
- Explain Augustine's privation account without making suffering unreal.
- Reconstruct Aquinas's direct and accidental causal distinctions.
- Compare both accounts with Lewis's treatment of wickedness and the fall.
- Test the historical positions against the canonical grammar from Week 2.

## Outline

1. Reading historical arguments without anachronism
2. Augustine's anti-dualist setting and the goodness of created natures
3. Evil as privation, corruption, and disordered will
4. Divine permission and ordering: what Augustine claims and what remains
5. Does privation deny genuine evil?
6. Aquinas's disputed-question method and three causal questions
7. Good as an accidental cause; defect in a secondary cause
8. God, fault, penalty, and the absence of a supreme evil principle
9. Lewis on concrete guilt, total depravity, the fall, and speculation
10. Canonical and confessional synthesis with safeguards against misuse

## Key terms

Nature; privation; corruption; vice; moral evil; natural evil; dualism; will;
permission; providence; direct cause; accidental cause; secondary cause; defect;
fault; penalty; original sin; total depravity; theodicy.
