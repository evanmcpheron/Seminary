# Week 1 Objectives — Framing Suffering and Evil

By the end of this week, the student will be able to:

1. distinguish pain, suffering, and evil without treating the terms as
   interchangeable (`LO-01`);
2. classify examples as moral evil, natural evil, or a causally mixed case and
   explain the limits of the classification (`LO-01`);
3. distinguish a logical problem from an evidential problem and a defense from
   a theodicy at an introductory level (`LO-01`);
4. reconstruct Lewis's opening account of how the Christian problem of pain
   arises and his account of omnipotence without overstating what either chapter
   establishes (`LO-01`, `LO-05`);
5. state a serious skeptical challenge to Christian theism in terms a
   responsible critic could recognize before offering evaluation (`LO-05`);
6. distinguish philosophical, theological, existential, and pastoral questions
   by their aims and standards of success (`LO-01`, `LO-07`); and
7. choose a non-coercive first response to a synthetic suffering case while
   respecting safety, privacy, competence, and referral boundaries (`LO-07`).

These objectives introduce categories that later weeks will test in greater
depth. Week 1 does not require the student to settle the problem of evil or to
disclose personal experience.
