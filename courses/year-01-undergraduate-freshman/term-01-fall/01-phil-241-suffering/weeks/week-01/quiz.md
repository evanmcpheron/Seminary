# Ungraded Retrieval Practice — Week 1

This is practice, not a quiz and not part of the course grade. Complete it
closed-note first. Then use the separate key to correct in a different color and
revisit the cited lecture or source location. Do not submit the key as your work.

## Time plan

| Stage | Time |
|---|---:|
| Closed-note first attempt | 0.40 h |
| Key review and corrections | 0.25 h |
| Source recheck for missed items | 0.30 h |
| Second retrieval and error log | 0.55 h |
| **Total** | **1.50 h** |

## Part A: terms and distinctions

1. In two sentences, distinguish pain, suffering, and evil.
2. Classify the following and explain: a wildfire starts by lightning, but
   officials knowingly disabled the warning system.
3. Why does “natural evil” not mean “morally acceptable”?
4. State the difference between the conclusion sought by a logical argument
   from evil and an evidential argument from evil.
5. State the difference between a defense and a theodicy.
6. What does “apparently gratuitous evil” mean at this introductory stage?

## Part B: sources and argument

7. List the four stages or strands Lewis uses in chapter 1 to explain how the
   distinctively Christian problem of pain arises.
8. In your own words, state Lewis's central claim about omnipotence in chapter 2.
9. Does Lewis's account of omnipotence prove that preventing any particular
   evil is contradictory? Explain.
10. What conception of God makes Peterson's philosophical problem especially
    pressing?
11. Reconstruct this as a three- or four-premise argument: “The actual pattern
    of apparently pointless suffering is more surprising on standard theism
    than on a rival view; therefore it counts as evidence against standard
    theism.”

## Part C: judgment

12. A person says, “I need you to show me exactly why belief in God is still
    rational.” Which lens should lead, and what clarifying question would you
    ask?
13. A bereaved person says, “Please do not explain this.” Give one appropriate
    first response and one response to avoid.
14. Name two triggers for leaving ordinary discussion and involving appropriate
    local emergency, safeguarding, pastoral, or licensed professional support.
15. Explain why “God can bring good from evil” does not license the statement
    “I know God caused this event to produce this good.”

## Correction protocol

After the first attempt, open
[the instructor key](instructor-materials/retrieval-practice-key.md). For each
missed or partial item, record:

- the corrected answer;
- the lecture heading or source location that supports it; and
- the confusion that caused the error.

Close the key, wait at least ten minutes while rechecking sources, and answer
items 4, 5, 8, 11, and 15 again from memory.
