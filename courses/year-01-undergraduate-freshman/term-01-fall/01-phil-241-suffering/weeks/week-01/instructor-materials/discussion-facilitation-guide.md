# Instructor Guide — Week 1 Guided Discussion

## Completion standard

The exercise is complete when the student has:

- stated both the skeptical challenge and introductory Lewisian response in
  recognizable form;
- distinguished what each establishes from what remains open;
- identified the leading lens and speech act in all three cases; and
- proposed responses that respect consent, safety, competence, and referral
  boundaries.

No grade is assigned. Correct conceptual errors immediately; do not score the
student's emotional reaction or religious confidence.

## Case anchors

- **Case A:** Philosophical inquiry leads because Alex requests premise-level
  evaluation. Clarify the premise before replying. Motive attribution and a
  monologue fail the charity standard.
- **Case B:** Existential and pastoral concerns lead. Acknowledge the loss and
  respect the refusal of argument. A later philosophical conversation requires
  invitation; no participant may name a hidden divine purpose.
- **Case C:** Immediate safety leads. Connect Riley with appropriate local
  emergency or safeguarding support and follow applicable reporting duties.
  Do not promise absolute secrecy, conduct an amateur investigation, or let the
  classroom exercise delay help.

## Facilitation cautions

- Redirect any real disclosure away from group analysis and toward private,
  appropriate support.
- Do not require prayer, testimony, or disclosure as evidence of completion.
- If a participant becomes overwhelmed, pause the exercise, preserve choice,
  and offer appropriate support options.
- This guide is educational and does not replace medical, mental-health, legal,
  emergency, or jurisdiction-specific safeguarding guidance.

## Debrief prompts

1. Where did timing change whether a true statement was wise to say?
2. Which response most clearly confused possibility with knowledge of an actual
   divine reason?
3. Did either restatement use language its proponent would reject?
4. What question best restored the speaker's agency?
