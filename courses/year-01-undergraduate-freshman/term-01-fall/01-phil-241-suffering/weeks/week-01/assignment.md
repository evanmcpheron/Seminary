# A-01 Reading and Argument Journal 1

## Purpose

Demonstrate that you can identify and reconstruct a source's argument, formulate
a charitable objection, and distinguish philosophical significance from
pastoral use. This is the first of twelve journal entries comprising A-01.

## Task

Choose one claim from Lewis chapter 1, Lewis chapter 2, or Peterson's
introduction. Write **300–400 words** under these four headings:

1. **Claim:** State the author's claim accurately in one or two sentences.
2. **Reasons:** Explain at least two reasons or steps supporting it.
3. **Objection:** State the strongest concise objection you can formulate. Do
   not question the author's or a reader's intelligence, faith, or motives.
4. **Significance:** Explain why the dispute matters and give one sentence on
   how the claim's classroom use could differ from its use in first pastoral
   response to a suffering person.

Use paraphrase. A direct quotation is not required. If you quote, keep it brief
and provide the exact page or licensed digital location from the edition you
used. Do not invent pagination.

## Required source reference

End with one short-form entry identifying author, abbreviated title,
chapter/introduction, and page or stable digital location when available. The
entry must allow the evaluator to find the passage in the student's lawful copy.
