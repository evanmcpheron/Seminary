# Week 1 Required Reading and Source Study

Use a lawful purchased, library, or licensed copy. Do not use or share an
unauthorized scan. Page references for Lewis are intentionally omitted because
pagination varies; the chapter titles are verified. The Peterson range is
verified for the assigned second edition.

## Required sources and time budget

| Source | Assignment | Guided source-study task | Time |
|---|---|---|---:|
| C. S. Lewis, *The Problem of Pain* | Chapter 1, “Introductory” | Mark the stages in Lewis's account of how the distinctively Christian problem arises. In the margin, separate historical description, philosophical reasoning, and Christian claim. | 1.25 h |
| Lewis, *The Problem of Pain* | Chapter 2, “Divine Omnipotence” | State Lewis's definition of omnipotence in your own words. Record one example he uses, one inference he draws, and one question his account leaves open. | 1.25 h |
| Michael L. Peterson, ed., *The Problem of Evil: Selected Readings*, 2nd ed. | “Introduction: The Problem of Evil,” 1–14 | Build a one-page map of the problem's principal forms and response strategies. Note where Peterson describes rather than endorses a position. | 1.50 h |
|  | **Total** |  | **4.00 h** |

The annotation and mapping are part of source study, not additional work beyond
the four-hour reading budget. Bring the notes to the guided activities; they are
not separately graded.

## Reading questions

1. Why, on Lewis's account, is pain a special intellectual problem only after
   certain beliefs about ultimate reality are in place?
2. What does Lewis include within omnipotence, and what does he exclude as
   meaningless rather than merely difficult?
3. Which part of either reading states the strongest challenge to theism?
4. Which distinctions clarify the discussion, and which might hide morally
   important complexity?
5. What would be lost if the problem were treated only as an abstract puzzle?
