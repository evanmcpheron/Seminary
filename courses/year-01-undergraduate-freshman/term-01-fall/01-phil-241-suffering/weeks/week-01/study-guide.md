# Week 1 Study Guide — Source and Concept Map

Complete this guide after the readings and lectures. It is ungraded guided
practice. Use only a synthetic or public example; do not record personal trauma
or another person's private information.

## Time plan

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Concept classification | 0.40 h |
| Source claim maps | 0.50 h |
| Four-question sorting | 0.35 h |
| Review and error log | 0.25 h |
| **Total** | **1.50 h** |

## 1. Concept classification — 24 minutes

For each case, identify the best initial category and justify it in one or two
sentences. More than one category may apply; distinguish the harm, its cause,
and any blameworthy agency.

1. A patient freely consents to a painful but beneficial procedure.
2. A manager knowingly conceals a workplace hazard, and an employee is injured.
3. An earthquake damages two buildings; corrupt inspection made one collapse.
4. A person enjoys spreading a humiliating lie.
5. A wild animal suffers from disease without identifiable human involvement.

Use these prompts:

- Is there pain, suffering, evil, or more than one?
- Is the evil moral, natural, or causally mixed?
- What additional facts would be needed before assigning blame?

## 2. Source claim maps — 30 minutes

Complete one map for Lewis and one for Peterson. Paraphrase rather than copying.

Then label each statement in your map as a primary-source claim, your inference,
or an evaluation. Do not present your inference as the author's statement.

## 3. Four-question sorting — 21 minutes

Read the synthetic statement: “After the fire, I cannot worship a God who could
have stopped it. Please do not preach at me; I need to know where my family can
sleep tonight.”

Write one sentence under each heading.

1. **Philosophical:** What argument or evidential question is present?
2. **Theological:** Which doctrines are implicated, and what must not be claimed
   without evidence?
3. **Existential:** What protest or crisis of integrity is expressed?
4. **Pastoral:** What should lead the first response, and what practical or
   safety boundary matters?

Finish by identifying one response that might be appropriate later but would be
a category mistake as the first reply.

## 4. Review and error log — 15 minutes

Without looking, define these pairs aloud or in writing:

- pain / suffering;
- moral evil / natural evil;
- logical problem / evidential problem;
- defense / theodicy;
- existential question / pastoral question.

Check your answers against the lectures. Record two corrections in this form:

> I first wrote ____. The more precise account is ____ because ____.

Retain the error log for cumulative retrieval work. Do not submit this guide for
a grade.
