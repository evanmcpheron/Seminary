## Claim audit by lecture

### Lecture 1

- Working definitions are explicitly presented as working categories, not as a
  single uncontested theory of evil.
- Moral/natural classification is supported and its mixed-case limitation is
  stated.
- Logical/evidential and defense/theodicy distinctions are introductory and do
  not claim that later disputes have been settled.
- Lewis's claims are separated from curriculum inference and confessional
  conclusion.
- Scripture references are not used to assign a specific cause to a sufferer's
  experience.

### Lecture 2

- Peterson supports the classical-theist framing and the logical, evidential,
  and existential map; the lecture does not attribute its four-lens pedagogical
  synthesis to Peterson.
- The skeptical challenge is presented as an evidential argument rather than an
  emotional defect.
- Pastoral guidance is expressly educational, non-diagnostic, and subject to
  local emergency, safeguarding, legal, pastoral, and licensed-professional
  boundaries.
- The synthetic case contains no private information and does not ask for
  student self-disclosure.
- Confessional conclusions are identified as this curriculum's conclusions,
  not as official material of any institution.
