# Lecture 1 Outline — Concepts and Distinctions

## Objectives

- Distinguish pain, suffering, and evil.
- Classify moral, natural, and mixed cases without confusing cause and blame.
- Distinguish logical and evidential questions, and defense and theodicy.
- Reconstruct the opening moves in Lewis and Peterson charitably.

## Outline

1. Why vocabulary is ethically and intellectually important
2. Pain, suffering, and evil
3. Moral evil, natural evil, and mixed causal histories
4. The object of the problem: standard theism and actual evils
5. Logical versus evidential formulations
6. Defense, theodicy, and protest
7. Lewis on the genesis of the problem and divine omnipotence
8. A confessional starting point that leaves room for lament

## Key terms

Pain; suffering; evil; moral evil; natural evil; mixed case; standard theism;
logical problem; evidential problem; defense; theodicy; apparently gratuitous
evil; omnipotence; contradiction; lament.
