# Lecture 2 Outline — Mackie's Challenge and Theistic Replies

## Objectives

- Explain Mackie's distinction between adequate and internally unstable
  solutions.
- Reconstruct the strongest form of his criticism of four common replies.
- Distinguish revising theism, defending consistency, and offering a theodicy.
- Explain how a possible morally sufficient reason blocks a strict
  contradiction.
- Assess the remaining objections involving moral and natural evil, heaven,
  divine freedom, and evidential force.
- Offer a modest confessional judgment without declaring the problem of evil
  finished.

## Outline

1. Reading Mackie as an internal critic
2. Adequate solutions and the cost of premise revision
3. Why verbal preservation can conceal substantive surrender
4. Good and evil as counterparts
5. Evil as a means, higher-order goods, and moral limits
6. Free will in Mackie's critique
7. Defense, theodicy, and the consistency-model strategy
8. Beebe's presentation of the free-will defense
9. Objections from natural evil, heaven, divine freedom, and evidence
10. What the logical debate settles and leaves open
11. Biblical-confessional and pastoral evaluation

## Key terms

Internal critique; adequate solution; internally unstable solution; first-order
good; higher-order good; means; necessary condition; free will; determinism;
morally significant freedom; morally sufficient reason; consistency model;
defense; theodicy; moral evil; natural evil; logical defeat; evidential problem.
