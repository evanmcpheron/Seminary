# Instructor Grading Guide — A-03 Argument Reconstruction Brief

Use the public 100-point rubric in `../rubric.md`. This guide clarifies the
acceptable range and protects student authorship; it does not provide a model
brief or canonical premise map.

## Threshold checks

Before scoring, confirm that:

- the brief is 750–950 words under the stated exclusions;
- all ten required sections are present;
- Philo is named as the immediate speaker and Cleanthes's empirical inference
  as the immediate target;
- the assigned Hume location is locatable;
- D01 and D02 process files are preserved;
- the change log records at least three material revisions; and
- metadata, sources, and AI-use declaration are complete.

A minor threshold defect normally affects the final criterion. Fabricated
evidence, a concealed externally generated map, or prohibited AI authorship is
an integrity issue rather than an ordinary deduction.

## Acceptable reconstruction range

Reasonable maps may differ in granularity. They will normally connect:

- the empirical method's requirement that inferred qualities be proportioned
  to observed effects;
- the world's mixed goods and evils;
- the inability of unknown reconciling reasons to become positive empirical
  evidence merely by being possible; and
- a conclusion limiting inference from the observed world to perfect goodness.

Some students may treat Philo's four circumstances as support for the
mixed-effects premise; others may place one circumstance in the main map.
Either is acceptable if the source relation and inferential role are clear.

Return or score down a map that converts Philo's argument into the claim that
God and evil are directly contradictory. A sound Week 4 revision should use
Mackie and Beebe to classify the argument, not substitute their argument for
Philo's.

## Objection and reply range

Accept objections concerning the restriction to observed effects, alternative
evidence for divine goodness, comparison of hypotheses, or the move from mixed
effects to limited moral inference. The objection must reach the student's map.

Accept an Augustine, Aquinas, Leibniz, revealed-theology, or consistency reply
when accurately represented. The highest-scoring responses state whether the
reply challenges a premise, limits the inference, introduces independent
evidence, or merely establishes compatibility. Barely possible reconciliation
does not by itself defeat Philo's evidential pressure.

## Scoring cautions

- Do not require one exact premise count beyond the published 5–7 range.
- Do not award accuracy points for a quotation that is irrelevant to the map.
- Do not penalize the student for rejecting the confessional conclusion.
- Do not treat “valid” as a general compliment; require the student to explain
  whether the map is deductive or evidential.
- Do not infer plagiarism or AI use from polish alone. Evaluate process
  evidence and the declaration under the course policy.

## Feedback boundary

Use comments that identify a criterion without supplying the student's work:

- “Which speaker and target authorize premise __?”
- “Is this step a source claim or your reconstruction?”
- “Does this objection challenge possibility or evidential support?”
- “Which exact premise does the reply address?”
- “What conclusion is stronger than your premises warrant?”

Do not write a replacement map, supply the suppressed premise, choose the
objection or reply, or rewrite evaluative prose.

## Retake

If the score is below 73, assign targeted remediation using the deficient
rubric criteria. One structured retake may be offered. Retain the original
submission and score, remediation record, retake, and final progression
decision.
