## Part I — 8 points

1. **B** — Consistency is possible joint truth, not actual truth or
   plausibility.
2. **C** — The initial claims are not direct negations; connecting premises are
   required.
3. **C** — This line connects perfect goodness to prevention while preserving
   a moral qualification.
4. **A** — Soundness is validity plus true premises.
5. **C** — An adequate solution genuinely rejects or qualifies a member of the
   inconsistent set.
6. **B** — A defense defeats an objection without claiming an actual divine
   reason; a theodicy proposes such an account.
7. **C** — One coherent countermodel defeats the claim of necessary
   incompatibility, and no stronger listed conclusion follows.
8. **C** — Week 4 directly develops Week 3's distinction between compatibility
   and evidential support.

## Part II — 12 points

### Item 9 — 6 points

- **Direct-contradiction diagnosis, 2 points:** neither line is the explicit
  negation of the other. A derivation connecting attributes to the nonexistence
  of evil is needed.
- **Bridge premise, 2 points:** accept a precise principle such as “A perfectly
  good being prevents every evil it knows and can prevent” or a comparably
  explicit conjunction of knowledge, ability, and goodness.
- **Defender's challenge, 2 points:** accept a claim that perfect goodness may
  permit an evil for a morally sufficient reason, or that omnipotence does not
  include contradictory tasks, provided the student explains which bridge is
  qualified. Denying evil does not satisfy the instruction.

### Item 10 — 6 points

- **First scope error, 3 points:** possible coexistence does not prove theism is
  true. It establishes at most that the described claims are not logically
  incompatible. Truth and overall warrant require different arguments.
- **Second scope error, 3 points:** a model tied to morally significant
  creaturely choice does not by itself explain natural evil. Natural-evil
  causation and any actual morally sufficient reason require further argument.
