## A-01-J04 — Reading and Argument Journal 4

**Parent assessment:** A-01 Reading and argument journals

**Status:** graded, 10 points within the twelve-entry A-01 series

**Time budget:** 0.50 hour

**Outcomes:** `LO-01`, `LO-03`, `LO-05`

### Purpose

Represent one part of the Mackie–Beebe exchange accurately and distinguish a
formal conclusion from a larger explanatory or pastoral claim.

### Task

Write **300–400 words** under five headings:

1. **Focused source claim:** Select one claim from Mackie's assigned chapter or
   one section of Beebe's entry. State it precisely and provide the assigned
   source location.
2. **Logical role:** Explain whether the claim is an initial proposition,
   bridge principle, definition, premise revision, countermodel, or objection.
   Show how it functions rather than merely naming it.
3. **Strong pressure:** Present the strongest source-relevant objection or
   limitation. If the target is a theistic reply, represent the reply in a form
   its defender could recognize before criticizing it.
4. **Scope control:** State exactly what would follow if the claim or objection
   succeeds and one larger conclusion that would not follow.
5. **Provisional judgment:** State what the argument clarifies and one logical,
   evidential, theological, or pastoral question it leaves open.

Use paraphrase. A direct quotation is not required. If quoting, keep the
quotation brief and cite the exact anthology page or Beebe section. Do not
claim that a possible morally sufficient reason is God's actual reason for a
particular person's suffering.

## A-03 — Argument Reconstruction Brief

**Status:** graded course assessment, 100 points, 10 percent of the course grade

**Time budget this week:** 1.50 hours

**Cumulative A-03 budget:** 5.00 hours: Week 2 development 1.50 hours; Week 3

### Purpose

Revise the Week 3 source-controlled map of Philo's argument into a concise
brief, using Week 4's formal distinctions to identify the argument's actual
kind, strength, and limits. This is an argument-reconstruction assessment, not
a confessional-position paper.

### Submission structure

Write **750–950 words**, excluding metadata, numbered premise map, notes,
bibliography, AI-use declaration, and change-log appendix. Use these headings:

1. **Argument identity and target** — Name Philo as the immediate speaker,
   identify Cleanthes's empirical inference as the immediate target, and state
   the relevant assigned-edition location.
2. **Numbered reconstruction** — Give 5–7 premises and a conclusion. Include no
   quotation unless brief and precisely located. Label every added premise
   “reconstructed.”
3. **Logical classification** — Explain whether the argument asserts a strict
   contradiction or makes an evidential inference. Use *validity* only if the
   reconstruction is deductive; otherwise analyze the strength of support.
4. **Ambiguity or suppressed premise** — Explain how one issue affects the
   inference rather than merely listing it.
5. **Strong objection** — Present the best pressure against the actual map.
6. **Theistic reply and point of contact** — Use one Week 3 historical reply or
   Week 4 consistency distinction. State exactly which premise, inference, or
   conclusion it addresses and whether it fully answers the argument.
7. **Preliminary evaluation and scope** — Give a reasoned judgment about the
   argument's strength and name at least one conclusion it does not establish.
8. **Sources actually consulted** — Supply SBL/Chicago-compatible notes and a
   bibliography for every source used.
9. **AI-use declaration** — Use the policy below.
10. **Change-log appendix** — List at least three material revisions from D02
    and the source, feedback, or reasoning that prompted each.

The brief should be concise and analytic. It need not defend Christianity or
agree with the curriculum's confessional conclusion. It must represent the
source accurately and evaluate the reasoning.
