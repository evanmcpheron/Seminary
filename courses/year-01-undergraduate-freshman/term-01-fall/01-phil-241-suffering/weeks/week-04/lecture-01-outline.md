# Lecture 1 Outline — Formal Consistency and the Logical Problem of Evil

## Objectives

- Distinguish truth, consistency, validity, soundness, possibility, and
  probability.
- Identify the difference between a direct contradiction and an indirectly
  inconsistent set.
- Reconstruct Mackie's core claims and make their bridge principles explicit.
- Test ambiguity in the claims about goodness and omnipotence.
- Explain the minimum burden carried by a consistency defense.
- Preserve the distance between a formal result and a pastoral response.

## Outline

1. The Week 3 handoff: compatibility is not evidential support
2. Direct contradiction and indirect inconsistency
3. Mackie's initial triad and the missing derivation
4. Bridge principles about goodness, power, knowledge, and prevention
5. A valid reconstruction of the logical argument
6. Scope, ambiguity, and counterexamples
7. Consistency models and morally sufficient reasons
8. What possibility can establish—and what it cannot
9. Confessional commitments under logical examination
10. Formal rigor with pastoral restraint

## Key terms

Proposition; conjunction; contradiction; direct contradiction; consistency;
indirect inconsistency; entailment; validity; soundness; necessary condition;
sufficient condition; counterexample; logical possibility; probability;
bridge principle; omnipotence; omniscience; perfect goodness; morally sufficient
reason; defense; theodicy.
