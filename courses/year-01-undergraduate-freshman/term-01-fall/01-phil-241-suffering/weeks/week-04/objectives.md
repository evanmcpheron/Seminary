# Week 4 Objectives — The Logical Problem of Evil

By the end of this week, the student will be able to:

1. distinguish a direct contradiction from an indirectly inconsistent set and
   identify the added premises needed to derive a contradiction (`LO-01`,
   `LO-02`);
2. reconstruct Mackie's logical argument from divine omnipotence, perfect
   goodness, and the existence of evil without treating the initial triad as a
   formally explicit contradiction (`LO-01`, `LO-02`, `LO-05`);
3. test the scope and ambiguity of Mackie's bridge principles concerning
   goodness, preventable evil, and omnipotence (`LO-01`, `LO-02`);
4. distinguish an adequate revision of a premise, an internally unstable reply,
   a defense, and a theodicy (`LO-01`, `LO-03`);
5. explain why a logically possible morally sufficient reason can answer a
   strict inconsistency charge without proving that the reason is actual,
   probable, or pastorally sufficient (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
6. present Mackie's objections and Beebe's account of theistic replies
   charitably before offering confessional evaluation (`LO-02`, `LO-03`,
   `LO-05`); and
7. differentiate defeat of the logical problem from resolution of the later
   evidential, existential, natural-evil, and pastoral problems (`LO-01`,
   `LO-02`, `LO-03`).

These objectives concern philosophical arguments. They do not authorize
identifying God's reason for a particular person's suffering, treating a
sufferer as an argument specimen, or assuming that logical consistency removes
the obligation to lament, protect, repair, and seek justice.
