# Week 4 Guided Discussion — The Consistency Hearing

This is 60 minutes of ungraded oral practice. The aim is to determine exactly
what the logical argument and its replies establish. No confessional agreement
or personal disclosure is required.

If no group is available, record each timed role privately and complete the
same audit. The recording is not submitted.

## Time plan

| Stage | Time |
|---|---:|
| Role and source preparation | 0.17 h |
| Mackie reconstruction hearing | 0.25 h |
| Countermodel hearing | 0.25 h |
| Scope verdict and revision | 0.33 h |
| **Total** | **1.00 h** |

## 1. Role and source preparation — 10 minutes

Assign four functions. Combine adjacent roles in smaller groups.

- **Mackie advocate:** prepares the initial claims, bridge principles, and one
  criticism of a theistic reply.
- **Consistency defender:** prepares a morally qualified prevention principle
  and one minimal possible countermodel.
- **Logical auditor:** checks definitions, quantifiers, validity, and whether a
  reply changes a premise.
- **Scope and pastoral auditor:** identifies moves from possibility to
  actuality or probability and stops use of the formal argument as a diagnosis
  of a real person's suffering.

Each role records one claim, one reason, one objection, and one assigned-source
location. Before disagreeing, each advocate must restate the opposing claim and
receive confirmation that it is recognizable.

## 2. Mackie reconstruction hearing — 15 minutes

The Mackie advocate presents a numbered argument in no more than three minutes.
The logical auditor then asks:

1. Where is the direct contradiction, or what steps derive it?
2. Which bridge premise connects goodness to prevention?
3. Which bridge premise connects omnipotence to ability?
4. Where does knowledge enter the reconstruction?
5. Are *every*, *can*, and *must* used consistently?

The consistency defender may challenge only one premise at a time. The Mackie
advocate must give the strongest available reply from the assigned article.
The scope auditor classifies each exchange as definition, validity, or premise
truth.

## 3. Countermodel hearing — 15 minutes

The defender presents a possible model involving morally significant freedom.
The Mackie advocate tests it with four pressures:

- Why could free agents not always choose rightly?
- Is God unable to secure the good without the evil?
- Does the model address natural evil?
- Does the model establish an actual reason or merely a possible one?

The defender may answer the logical-possibility issue but must concede any
evidential, theological, or natural-evil question the model has not resolved.
The logical auditor issues a provisional ruling: **contradictory**, **not yet
shown possible**, or **possible for purposes of the hearing**. Reasons are
required.

## 4. Scope verdict and revision — 20 minutes

The group evaluates four proposed conclusions:

1. “Theism is logically impossible.”
2. “Theism has been proved true.”
3. “The classical logical inconsistency charge has a possible answer.”
4. “The whole problem of evil has been solved.”

For each, mark **established**, **not established**, or **requires a different
argument**. Cite the exact result of the two hearings.

Then consider a fictional setting: a student asks in a philosophy seminar
whether a consistency defense answers a deductive objection; later, a friend
reports a recent bereavement. Identify why the same sentence may be relevant in
the first setting and harmful or premature in the second. Do not invent the
cause of the loss or require anyone to imitate the bereaved person.

End with a four-sentence joint verdict:

1. Mackie's strongest formal contribution;
2. the defender's strongest logical reply;
3. the most important unresolved objection; and
4. the pastoral boundary on using the result.

Each participant privately revises one earlier sentence that overstated either
side.
