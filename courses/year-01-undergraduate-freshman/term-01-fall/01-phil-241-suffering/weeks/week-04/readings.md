## Reading questions

1. Why do the claims “God is omnipotent,” “God is perfectly good,” and “evil
   exists” not display a direct contradiction without further premises?
2. Which bridge principles does Mackie add, and what scope does each need for
   his conclusion?
3. What does Mackie count as an adequate solution, and why might it carry a
   substantial theological cost?
4. Why does Mackie regard some responses as unstable rather than as genuine
   rejections of a premise?
5. What would a consistency model need to show in order to answer the logical
   problem?
6. Why is a possibly true morally sufficient reason enough for that limited
   task but not enough for an actual theodicy?
7. Which objections remain after logical compatibility is established?
8. How does Week 3's compatibility/evidence distinction prevent both a
   premature skeptical victory and a premature theistic victory?
