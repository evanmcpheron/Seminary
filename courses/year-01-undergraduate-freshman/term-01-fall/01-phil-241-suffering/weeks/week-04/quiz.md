# A-02-Q02 — Retrieval Quiz 2

This is a **graded, cumulative, closed-book quiz** covering Weeks 1–4. Complete
it after all other Week 4 work.

## Conditions

- **Time:** 30 minutes total
- **Materials:** closed book, closed notes, no web, no AI, no assistance
- **Format:** eight multiple-choice items and two short argument-analysis items
- **Points:** 20 total
- **Submission:** submit the answers and the integrity statement when time ends

Spend about 10 minutes on Part I, 17 minutes on Part II, and 3 minutes checking
completion and signing the statement. Do not consult the instructor key.

## Part I — Concepts and distinctions, 8 points

Select the best answer. Each item is worth 1 point.

1. Which statement best defines a logically consistent set?

   A. Every statement in the set is actually true.
   B. The statements can all be true together.
   C. The set contains at least one valid argument.
   D. Every reasonable person finds the set plausible.

2. Mackie's initial claims need bridge principles because:

   A. the existence of evil is uncertain;
   B. omniscience is identical to omnipotence;
   C. none of the initial claims is a direct negation of another;
   D. only evidential arguments can be valid.

3. Which claim most clearly functions as a bridge principle in the logical
   problem?

   A. Evil exists.
   B. God is omniscient.
   C. A perfectly good being prevents every evil it can prevent unless
   permission is morally justified.
   D. Hume wrote a dialogue.

4. A valid argument is sound when:

   A. its premises are also true;
   B. its conclusion is emotionally satisfying;
   C. it contains no controversial term;
   D. it offers a complete theodicy.

5. In Mackie's classification, an adequate solution removes the alleged
   inconsistency by:

   A. refusing to state any premise;
   B. changing the subject to pastoral care;
   C. genuinely rejecting or qualifying a member of the inconsistent set;
   D. proving that every actual evil has a known purpose.

6. Which statement best distinguishes a defense from a theodicy?

   A. A defense must use Scripture, while a theodicy must not.
   B. A defense defeats an objection without claiming God's actual reason;
   a theodicy proposes an actual justifying account.
   C. A defense is deductive, while a theodicy is always inductive.
   D. There is no relevant distinction.

7. Which conclusion follows from one coherent consistency model?

   A. Theism is true.
   B. Every evil benefits its sufferer.
   C. God and evil are not logically incompatible merely as such.
   D. The evidential problem has been defeated.

8. Which Week 3 distinction is most directly carried into Week 4?

   A. Pain and suffering
   B. Moral evil and natural evil
   C. Compatibility and evidential support
   D. Fault and penalty

## Part II — Short argument analysis, 12 points

Answer each in no more than 120 words. Each item is worth 6 points.

9. Consider this set:

   1. God is omnipotent, omniscient, and perfectly good.
   2. Evil exists.

   Explain why the two lines are not a direct contradiction. Add one plausible
   bridge premise that would help derive a contradiction, then state one
   precise way a defender could challenge that bridge without denying that
   evil exists.

10. A student writes: “The free-will defense describes one possible morally
    sufficient reason. Therefore it proves that God exists and explains all
    natural evil.” Identify **two distinct scope errors**. For each, state the
    limited conclusion a consistency defense may support and name the different
    question that remains.
