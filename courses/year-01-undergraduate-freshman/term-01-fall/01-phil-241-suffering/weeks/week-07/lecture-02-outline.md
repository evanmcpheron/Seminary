# Lecture 2 Outline — Historical and Logical Synthesis

## Objectives

- Organize Weeks 1–7 by question, argumentative task, and conclusion strength.
- Reconstruct the logical problem and a consistency defense in valid form.
- Compare privation, causal, possible-world, dialogical, free-will,
  stable-order, and eschatological responses.
- Distinguish historical relation from simple chronological progress.
- Integrate biblical sources without using confessional premises to evade a
  shared philosophical question.
- Identify what remains unresolved when logical consistency is established.

## Outline

1. Synthesis as disciplined distinction
2. Four questions hidden within “the problem of evil”
3. Augustine: evil as corruption of created good
4. Aquinas: causal precision and creaturely defect
5. Leibniz: complete worlds and comparative goodness
6. Hume: dialogue, evidence, and inference
7. Mackie: bridge principles and strict inconsistency
8. Plantinga: possible and feasible worlds
9. Swinburne and Stump: natural evil, knowledge, and alternatives
10. Lewis: cumulative Christian interpretation
11. Scripture as canon, evidence, and confessional norm
12. A first-half decision tree and unresolved questions

Privation; accidental cause; possible world; feasible world; compatibility;
evidential support; bridge principle; logical inconsistency; defense;
theodicy; transworld depravity; stable order; necessary condition; comparative
superiority; general claim; kind-level claim; token explanation; cumulative
