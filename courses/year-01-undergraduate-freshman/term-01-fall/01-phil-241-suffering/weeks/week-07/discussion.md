# Week 7 Guided Discussion — Does Heaven Answer the Problem?

This is **15 minutes of ungraded guided practice**. Complete it with a partner
or as a recorded self-dialogue. Discuss only the course arguments; no personal
disclosure is requested.

## Question

Lewis argues that a Christian account of suffering is incomplete without
heaven. Does adding final communion and glory answer the problem, relocate it,
or do both? Give the strongest answer on each side before reaching a limited
conclusion.

## Roles

- **Constructive interpreter:** explain why eschatology belongs internally to
  Christian theism and why the good Lewis describes is not merely an external
  prize.
- **Critical analyst:** press necessity, feasible alternatives, amount,
  distribution, beneficiary, and the difference among compensation,
  outweighing, and defeat.
- **Scope keeper:** identify what Romans 8 and Revelation 21–22 support and
  what token inference they do not authorize.

For a self-dialogue, give each role an uninterrupted turn.

## Protocol

1. **Prepare — 3 minutes.** Each role writes one claim, one reason, and one
   conclusion that exceeds the available support.
2. **Exchange — 8 minutes.** The constructive interpreter speaks for two
   minutes, the critic for two, and the scope keeper for two. Use the remaining
   two minutes for replies that name an exact premise or inference.
3. **Synthesize — 4 minutes.** Produce a three-sentence joint verdict: what
   chapter 10 adds; what it does not prove; and what duties remain toward a
   sufferer under uncertainty.

## Completion standard

The exercise is complete when each position has been stated charitably, at
least one objection names its point of contact, the joint verdict distinguishes
hope from token explanation, and no one uses eschatology to discourage lament,
protection, treatment, truth-telling, justice, or care.
