# Week 7 Objectives — First-Half Synthesis

By the end of this week, the student will be able to:

1. define and relate the principal concepts used in Weeks 1–7, including
   *defense*, *theodicy*, *privation*, *bridge principle*, *possible world*,
   *feasible world*, *natural evil*, *outweighing*, *compensation*, and
   *defeat* (`LO-01`);
2. reconstruct a strict logical argument from evil with its bridge principles
   and explain exactly what a Plantinga-style consistency defense establishes
   and leaves open (`LO-01`, `LO-02`);
3. distinguish the questions answered by Augustine, Aquinas, Leibniz, Hume,
   Mackie, Plantinga, Swinburne, Stump, and Lewis without treating their
   positions as interchangeable (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
4. assess Lewis's cumulative account of omnipotence, goodness, human
   wickedness, fall, human pain, hell, animal pain, and heaven as a popular
   Christian theodicy whose arguments, speculations, and admitted limits must
   be evaluated separately (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
5. explain Lewis's chapter 10 appeal to heaven as an eschatological horizon,
   then test whether future glory amounts to compensation, outweighing,
   defeat, or a further claim needing argument (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
6. use the completed comparison of Job 1–2 and 38–42, Romans 8:18–39, and
   other Week 2 texts to distinguish canonical hope from an unsupported
   explanation of a particular sufferer's loss (`LO-04`);
7. write a cumulative, closed-book analysis that is accurate in definition,
   attribution, argument structure, objection fit, biblical context, and
   conclusion scope (`LO-01`, `LO-02`, `LO-03`, `LO-04`); and
8. frame a provisional comparison question for A-05 that places one argument
   from evil in conversation with two materially different responses without
   deciding the paper's conclusion in advance (`LO-02`, `LO-03`).

The week's synthesis and examination require no personal disclosure. No
philosophical or theological answer may be used to assign a hidden divine
purpose, guilt, or spiritual deficiency to a particular sufferer.
