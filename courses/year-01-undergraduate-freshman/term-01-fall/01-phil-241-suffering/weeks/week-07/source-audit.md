## Lewis chapter 10 audit

- The title “Heaven” and its position as chapter 10 were verified.
- Lewis's opening appeal to Romans 8 is represented as a methodological and
  theological claim that a Christian account of suffering must include its
  eschatological commitments.
- The lecture does not infer that heaven persuades an interlocutor who rejects
  Christian eschatology; it distinguishes internal worldview completeness
  from shared evidential warrant.
- Lewis's response to the “bribe” objection is paraphrased as a distinction
  between an external wage and fulfillment internal to love of its fitting
  object. No extended wording is reproduced.
- His account of a deep individualized longing is explicitly classified as
  personal opinion, in keeping with his own confidence marker.
- The images of a soul's unique fit, a city or body of distinct persons,
  reciprocal giving, and an eternal dance are treated as theological and
  imaginative proposals rather than literal maps or demonstrated mechanisms.
- Redeemed individuality is not absorbed into an aggregate. This supports a
  beneficiary test without proving that every sufferer receives a particular
  good through a particular evil.
- Self-giving is not converted into authorization for coerced loss,
  self-erasure, abuse, or refusal of protection.
- The audit found no complete Lewis argument proving that future glory makes
  every past evil necessary, proportionate, outweighed, compensated, or
  defeated. The lecture states that limitation explicitly.

## Cumulative Lewis audit

- Chapter 2's account of omnipotence is used to exclude contradictions from
  the objects of power; it is not made to determine which coherent world God
  ought to actualize.
- Chapter 3's analogies of love illustrate perfecting rather than merely
  indulgent goodness; they do not establish the necessity of a particular
  harm.
- Chapters 4–5 retain the difference among moral responsibility, Christian
  doctrine, and an imaginative picture of early human history.
- Chapters 6–7 are presented as proposals about possible or actual uses of
  human pain, with Lewis's controls that pain is not good in itself and that
  beneficial consequences do not justify a human wrongdoer.
- Chapter 8's treatment of judgment, refusal, finality, and imagery is not
  conflated with natural evil, and proportionality and grace remain live
  objections.
- Chapter 9's animal discussion remains explicitly speculative; uncertainty
  about animal consciousness is not used to deny apparent suffering, and the
  prehuman nonhuman-agency proposal is not reported as creed or science.
- Chapter 10 changes the interpretive horizon without retroactively raising
  the confidence of earlier hypotheses.

## Historical-source audit

- Augustine's privation account is not misreported as denial that injury or
  suffering is real. Its principal contribution concerns evil's ontological
  dependence on created good and the disorder of will.
- Aquinas is not reduced to divine absence from providence. Direct and
  accidental causation, creaturely defect, fault, and penalty are distinguished.
- Leibniz's whole-world comparison is separated from a bare compatibility
  defense. The lecture states rather than hides its burden concerning complete
  alternatives and persons within the whole.
- Claims in Hume's dialogue are attributed to their speaker, especially Philo;
  the lecture does not collapse compatibility, evidential support, and best
  explanation.
- Mackie's initial divine-attribute/evil triad is not called formally
  contradictory without bridge principles. The two added premises are visibly
  labeled simplified curriculum reconstruction.
- Plantinga's significant freedom, possible/feasible distinction, and possible
  transworld depravity are confined to the logical task. The lecture does not
  claim transworld depravity is actual, necessary, or a full account of
  natural evil.
- Swinburne's knowledge-and-choice proposal is distinguished from the stronger
  claims of necessity, comparative superiority, and justification of actual
  amount and distribution.
- Stump's necessity and alternative-means pressures are stated separately. She
  is not presented as refuting every possible stable-order or natural-evil
  account.

## Argument and value-relation audit

- Every numbered argument map is identified as curriculum reconstruction.
- *Possible*, *feasible*, *necessary*, *probable*, *actual*, and *known* are
  not treated as synonyms.
- A successful consistency defense is not said to establish the truth or
  probability of theism, God's actual reason, or the justification of every
  evil.
- Compensation, outweighing, and defeat are distinguished. None is attributed
  to Lewis as his explicit taxonomy.
- Amount, distribution, alternatives, beneficiary, agency, proportion, and
  epistemic access are retained as separate tests.
- The general/kind-level/token distinction continues the Week 6 audited
  analytical framework and is not falsely attributed to the historical
  authors.

## Scripture and confessional-position audit

- Scripture is identified as canonical norm within Christian theology and as
  a stated premise within a philosophical model. The lecture does not pretend
  that invoking canonical authority supplies shared warrant for every critic.
- Genesis 1–3 is used for created goodness and rebellion without assigning
  each natural process to a specific historical sin.
- Job's reader information and divine speeches are not turned into a reason
  available to Job. The friends' error constrains token diagnosis.
- Psalms 22, 73, and 88 authorize varied forms and trajectories of lament; the
  lecture does not require emotional resolution.
- Romans 8 joins groaning, the Spirit's aid, providence, inseparable love, and
  hoped-for liberation; it does not call suffering good or disclose why a
  token event occurred.
- Revelation 21–22 is used for judgment, renewed creation, divine presence, and
  the end of death and pain; it is not reduced to disembodied compensation.
- Confessional evaluation follows charitable reconstruction and does not
  require rejection of a student's faith or caricature of skeptical voices.

## Pastoral, integrity, and copyright audit

- No personal trauma disclosure is required.
- The lectures never assign a hidden divine purpose, guilt, deficient faith,
  or demonstrated nonhuman agency to a particular sufferer.
- No argument discourages medical or mental-health care, safety action,
  investigation, reporting, lament, justice, protection, animal care, or
  referral.
- The lectures distinguish analytical success from pastoral adequacy and make
  immediate duties independent of completing a theodicy.
- The text is original synthesis. It reproduces no extended passage from
  Lewis, Peterson, any anthology contributor, Beebe, Chisholm, or a copyrighted
  Bible edition.
