# Week 7 Required Reading and First-Half Source Review

Use the lawful purchased, library, licensed, or approved free access route
already specified in Weeks 1–6. The only new reading is C. S. Lewis, *The
Problem of Pain*, chapter 10. The remaining time is structured review of
sources already assigned and completed; it does not add a new chapter or page
range.

## Integrative reading questions

1. Which parts of Lewis's book function as consistency proposals, and which
   parts make stronger claims about God's actual reasons or purposes?
2. Where does chapter 10 state doctrine, and where does Lewis explicitly lower
   his confidence to personal opinion or metaphor?
3. If future glory is placed against present suffering, is the proposed
   relation compensation, outweighing, defeat, or something else?
4. Would an infinite or everlasting good automatically justify every route to
   it? What additional premises about necessity, agency, relation, and the
   sufferer's good are needed?
5. How do Augustine's account of evil's nature and Aquinas's causal analysis
   differ from a reason God might have for permitting evil?
6. Why does Leibniz's comparison of complete worlds not answer Hume's demand
   for evidence merely by restating compatibility?
7. Which bridge principles make Mackie's strict inconsistency argument work,
   and which premise does Plantinga's possible-world model challenge?
8. Why does a defense successful against logical inconsistency leave Humean,
   natural-evil, distributional, and pastoral questions open?
9. Which claims in Swinburne's account concern a stable order, and how do
   Stump's necessity and alternative-means objections differ?
10. How can Job and Romans 8 govern Christian hope without disclosing the
    reason for a particular injury or loss?
