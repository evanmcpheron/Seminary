# Lecture 1 Outline — Lewis's Theodicy in Critical Perspective

## Objectives

- Identify the argument, theological claim, illustration, speculation, and
  admitted limit in Lewis's cumulative project.
- Explain how chapter 10 makes heaven integral to Lewis's Christian response.
- Distinguish future compensation, outweighing, and defeat.
- Test Lewis's account for consistency, truth, scope, proportionality,
  distribution, beneficiary, and pastoral use.
- State both the genuine strengths and the unresolved objections in Lewis's
  approach.
- Relate Lewis's eschatological horizon to Romans 8 without turning hope into a
  token explanation.

## Outline

1. Reading Lewis at the end rather than in fragments
2. The kind of project Lewis undertakes
3. Omnipotence and coherent creaturely worlds
4. Divine goodness, human rebellion, and the fall
5. Human pain: possible uses and moral controls
6. Hell and animal pain: judgment and admitted speculation
7. Chapter 10: why heaven changes the frame
8. Desire, personhood, communion, and self-giving
9. Is future glory compensation, outweighing, or defeat?
10. Lewis's cumulative strengths
11. Seven unresolved critical pressures
12. Confessional reception and pastoral non-application

Popular theodicy; defense; theological claim; illustration; speculation;
