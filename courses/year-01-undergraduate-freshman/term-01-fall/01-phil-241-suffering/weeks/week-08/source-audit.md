## Argument and inference audit

- Rowe's 1979 compact form is correctly represented as a valid argument whose
  factual premise receives inductive support. It is not called a deductive
  proof that gratuitous suffering exists.
- The factual premise concerns at least some actual intense suffering. The
  theological premise concerns qualified prevention by an omniscient, wholly
  good being. Neither is replaced with the vague claim that suffering “has no
  point.”
- The greater-good and equal-or-worse-evil clauses remain distinct. A good's
  mere occurrence after suffering is not treated as the necessary relation
  Rowe's premise requires.
- Rowe's three conditions are treated as necessary conditions for justified
  nonprevention, not as a complete sufficient moral theory.
- The fawn example is described only to the extent needed for its
  argumentative role: natural causation, animal suffering, apparent absence of
  observer benefit, duration, and preventability. The lecture neither calls it
  an observed historical event nor reproduces the source narrative.
- Rowe's concession that unknown familiar or unfamiliar goods may exist is
  preserved. His conclusion concerns rational support rather than certainty.
- The movement from a particular case to the variety and profusion of suffering
  is identified as cumulative induction, not a hidden universal premise.
- The expanded appearance/factual/theological maps, detectability controls,
  four-part audit, and six-level conclusion scale are explicitly curriculum
  reconstructions rather than Rowe's words or numbering.

## Response and position audit

- A possible-world defense is credited with answering a strict consistency
  claim without being represented as a sufficient evidential answer.
- A direct attack may identify a good, challenge its necessity, or question the
  inductive support. The lecture preserves Rowe's pressure involving excess,
  natural evil, and token scope.
- The G. E. Moore shift is represented as an equally valid counterargument
  whose force depends on grounds for theism. It is not described as exposing a
  formal fallacy in Rowe.
- Evil's force in isolation is separated from an all-things-considered verdict.
  A counterevidence response may concede prima facie evidence without
  conceding the final conclusion.
- Undercutting and rebutting are distinguished. Cognitive limitation can
  weaken an inference without proving that a justifying reason exists.
- The later skeptical-theist debate and Draper's explanatory comparison are
  previewed but not preempted; their full evaluation remains assigned to Weeks
  9–10.
- Attribute-revision responses are required to disclose when they alter
  Rowe's standard-theism target.
- Rowe's friendly atheism is correctly treated as the conjunction of atheism
  with the judgment that some theists may be rationally justified. It is not
  called relativism or agreement with theism.

## Claim-label, confessional, and biblical audit

- Primary-source reports, broad taxonomy, contested inference, confessional
  conclusions, and curriculum synthesis are signaled where confusion is
  likely.
- The course's evangelical Protestant conclusion follows a fair presentation
  of Rowe rather than replacing it. The lectures do not imply that the Rowe
  argument is weak because its conclusion conflicts with the curriculum's
  confession.
- Job 38–42 and Romans 8:18–39 are reused through the passed Week 2 and Week 7
  audits. Job's friends are not vindicated, and Romans 8 does not call evil
  good or disclose a token reason.
- Romans 11:33–36, the lament psalms, Mark 15, and Revelation 21–22 are used at
  the level of established canonical themes, without an extended quotation,
  a novel translation claim, or an original-language claim.
- Creaturely limitation is allowed to constrain exhaustive judgment while also
  constraining the theodicist from assigning an undisclosed divine purpose.

## Pastoral, safety, and copyright audit

- The readings file warns about severe human and animal suffering. The student
  may omit graphic details of the IEP entry's second case without penalty and
  is never required to reproduce them.
- No task requires personal disclosure, research into an actual tragedy, or a
  judgment about why a known person suffered.
- A possible hidden divine reason is explicitly denied the status of human
  authorization. It cannot suspend protection, reporting, intervention,
  medical or mental health care, lament, justice, or practical support.
- Later goods do not excuse an abuser, negligent agent, or bystander. Divine
  permission and human duty are analyzed separately.
- The lectures, outlines, and source audit are original synthesis. They contain
  no extended passage from Rowe, Peterson, Trakakis, an encyclopedia, an
  anthology, or a copyrighted Bible edition.
