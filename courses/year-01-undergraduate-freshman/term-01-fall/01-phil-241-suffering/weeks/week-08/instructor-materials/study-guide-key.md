# Instructor Key — Week 8 Study Guide

This key supports self-correction of ungraded practice. Accept equivalent
precise answers. Do not turn these anchors into required prose for A-01-J07 or
A-05-P02.

## 1. Three-level claim sort

1. **Appearance.** This reports the outcome of inquiry.
2. **Factual premise.** This asserts the relevant gratuity relation actually
   obtains.
3. **Theological premise.** This connects perfect goodness and knowledge with
   qualified prevention.
4. **Atheistic conclusion.** More exactly, it is a probabilistic or rational-
   support conclusion in an evidential version.
5. **None/insufficient.** Existential response matters, but the feeling alone
   does not state one of the argument levels.
6. **None/insufficient.** Temporal sequence does not establish necessity,
   greater-good connection, moral adequacy, or worse-evil prevention.

Statements 1 and 2 differ because a failure to find a reason is compatible
with one existing; an inductive bridge is needed. Statement 6 may name a good,
but Rowe's condition asks whether preventing the suffering would lose the
good or require an equal or worse evil—not merely whether a good followed.

## 2. Compact and expanded maps

### Compact map anchor

1. Some intense suffering could be prevented by an omnipotent, omniscient
   being without loss of a greater good or permission of an equal or worse
   evil.
2. An omniscient, wholly good being prevents any intense suffering it can
   unless one of those qualifying conditions obtains.
3. Therefore, no omnipotent, omniscient, wholly good being exists.

The displayed compact inference is valid if its premises are true. The
evidential dispute centers on the inductive support for premise 1.

### Expanded-map anchor

A sound expanded map adds: no known qualifying good is apparent; a bridge
about the expected detectability or representativeness of relevant goods; an
inductive conclusion that at least some suffering is probably gratuitous; the
qualified-prevention premise; and the atheistic conclusion. Student wording
and the bridge must be labeled reconstruction.

The strongest conclusion depends on the map. Rowe argues for rational support
for atheism, not deductive certainty that no divine being of any kind exists.

## 3. Absence-inference controls

1. **Strong evidence.** A full-size chair is highly detectable under the stated
   conditions and ordinary search competence is adequate.
2. **No or very weak evidence.** Bacteria are not detectable with unaided
   vision.
3. **Moderate to strong evidence, qualified.** Appropriate training and method
   increase competence, though a fault might not store a code.
4. **Weak evidence.** A beginner has low expected competence relative to a
   complex position.
5. **Contested.** The cognitive-limit respondent stresses vast domain and
   competence gaps; Rowe's defender stresses human grasp of significant goods,
   moral relations, persistent careful reflection, and a broad sample.

Accept a range for items 3 and 5 if the detectability and competence reasons
are explicit.

## 4. Response role and conclusion audit

1. **Undercutting:** challenges the appearance-to-gratuity bridge.
2. **Rebutting:** supplies reason to deny gratuity, assuming necessity,
   adequacy, and beneficiary claims are defended.
3. **Counterevidence:** addresses the all-things-considered result rather than
   denying evil's isolated force.
4. **Target revision:** changes standard divine power unless the limitation is
   only logical and already part of the target.
5. **Incomplete:** bare possibility may answer proof but does not automatically
   remove evidential force.

Conclusion-scale anchors:

- relevance is level 1;
- “proves no God exists” is level 6 and is not warranted by Rowe's evidential
  case;
- prima facie evidence is level 2;
- a total-evidence atheistic verdict is level 5 and needs evidence excluded by
  the assigned isolated question; and
- uncertainty after undercutting usually supports suspension or a lower level,
  not a rebutting conclusion.

The final Christian-charity sentences should distinguish truth from rational
justification, acknowledge the evidence's seriousness, and avoid deciding the
argument by identity or motive.

## Feedback boundary

If an answer fails, identify its error category—definition, attribution,
inference, defeater, or scope—and direct the student to the relevant lecture
section. Do not compose a journal paragraph or fill the A-05 dossier.
