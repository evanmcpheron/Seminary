# Instructor Guide — A-05-P02 Evidential Argument Dossier

This guide supports an ungraded process milestone. It must not become a model
paper, completed dossier, source-selection service, or substitute for the
student's judgment.

## Completion audit

Mark complete only when the dossier includes:

- the Week 7 architecture preserved with every element marked `retain`,
  `revise`, or `defer` and revisions explained from evidence;
- a source-location sheet that distinguishes Rowe, Trakakis, and the student's
  reconstruction;
- a source-faithful compact map and a labeled expanded map;
- detectability, search-competence, domain, sample, and conclusion-ceiling
  analysis;
- two response profiles that differ in argumentative work;
- a strong counterargument with a precise point of contact and a limited
  reply;
- three comparison criteria, two genuine Week 9–10 questions, and a scheduled
  next action;
- a time record totaling no more than three hours; and
- an honest AI-use declaration.

Do not assign points or predict the later A-05 grade from completion.

## Accuracy audit

### Compact map

The map must retain:

1. at least some intense suffering meeting both gratuity conditions;
2. the qualified-prevention claim about an omniscient, wholly good being; and
3. the conclusion denying an omnipotent, omniscient, wholly good being.

It may call the displayed inference deductively valid while recognizing that
the factual premise receives inductive support.

### Expanded map

The expanded map should display failure to find a known qualifying good, the
bridge about expected detection or representativeness, the probable-gratuity
claim, the theological premise, and the limited atheistic conclusion. Do not
require one canonical wording. Require all added statements to be labeled as
student reconstruction.

### Response difference

Two author names do not guarantee two strategies. Substantial differences
include:

- identifying an actual good versus challenging the inference;
- undercutting Rowe versus offering counterevidence;
- retaining standard theism versus revising an attribute; or
- defending a general theodicy versus limiting conclusion scope.

If both profiles merely say “God might have a reason,” return the dossier for
revision.

## Diagnostic prompts

- “Is that Rowe's claim, Trakakis's exposition, or your reconstruction?”
- “Where is the greater-good or worse-evil condition?”
- “Why would the item be detectable if it existed?”
- “Does this response show the conclusion false or weaken its support?”
- “Are you weighing evil alone or total evidence?”
- “What does this strategy leave unresolved?”
- “Which Week 9 or Week 10 source is genuinely needed?”

Name the defect but do not fill the map or table. If a citation is not
verifiable in the student's lawful copy, require a stable chapter heading or
web section rather than inventing a page.

## Scope and sequence controls

Week 8 may identify Draper's explanatory comparison and skeptical theism as
future needs, but it may not claim conclusions from those uncompleted Week
9–10 readings. A dossier may retain another earlier argument as the parent
paper's candidate; Rowe must still be analyzed as this week's evidential
candidate.

The cumulative A-05 total must read 4.00 hours through Week 8. The facilitator
must not encourage extra research that defeats the approved workload or adds
unverified sources.

## AI, authorship, and safety boundary

AI may clarify instructions or critique a genuine student-created dossier
against the completion list. It may not choose the argument or responses,
create or repair maps, fill audit cells, formulate the counterargument or
reply, decide criteria, or draft prose. Require correction of an incomplete AI
declaration.

Do not request graphic case details, personal disclosure, or outside tragedy
research. A response using possible divine reasons to excuse human passivity
or assign a victim's purpose is incomplete and must be corrected at the scope
and ethics level.
