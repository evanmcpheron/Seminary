# Instructor Grading Guide — A-01-J07

Apply the student-facing rubric in `../rubric.md`. These anchors clarify
accuracy and scope; they are not a sample response and must not be supplied as
replacement prose.

## Source-control anchors

Accept Rowe's claim from his opening contribution in Peterson chapter 9 or an
accurate Trakakis exposition in section 2. The student must distinguish:

- Rowe as proponent from Trakakis as expositor;
- the complete chapter's three authors from the author of a selected claim;
- Rowe's 1979 compact argument from his later formulations; and
- an assigned chapter or stable heading from an invented Rowe-only page span.

The factual premise asserts that at least some intense suffering satisfies the
greater-good and equal-or-worse-evil conditions. The theological premise
states a qualified prevention expectation for an omniscient, wholly good
being. The compact inference is valid; the support for the factual premise is
inductive.

## Inductive-bridge anchors

Full-credit analysis need not use technical vocabulary beyond the lecture, but
it should ask whether a justifying good or relation would probably be
detectable if it existed. A reason for force may invoke careful reflection,
human grasp of great goods, persistent appearance, or sample variety and
profusion. A limit may invoke finite competence, incomplete domain coverage,
unknown goods, remote relations, or counterfactual complexity.

Do not require the student to decide the full Week 10 skeptical-theist debate.
The journal must state a provisional conclusion ceiling rather than pretend
later sources have been mastered.

## Response-role anchors

Accept a response that accurately performs one of these roles:

- **rebutting:** gives reason to think a qualifying justification exists;
- **undercutting:** weakens the appearance-to-gratuity inference;
- **counterevidence:** introduces independent grounds relevant to a total-
  evidence judgment;
- **moral-premise challenge:** disputes or qualifies the prevention principle;
  or
- **target revision:** changes an attribute within Rowe's standard-theism
  target and admits doing so.

A bare possible reason is insufficient unless the student explains its limited
undercutting relevance. A Moore shift is not a proof that Rowe's inference is
invalid. A later good is not automatically a necessary greater good.

## Charity, confessional, and safety boundaries

The student may affirm or reject Rowe's conclusion. Full credit requires
recognizing that a responsible person can be rationally justified while the
student judges that person's belief false. Do not reward confessional agreement
in place of analysis or penalize respectful disagreement.

Major errors include ridiculing faith or atheism, assigning a hidden divine
purpose, denying the evil of suffering because a good is possible, treating
cognitive limitation as proof that a reason exists, or using providence to
discourage protection, reporting, treatment, or care.

## Feedback and authorship boundary

Feedback may name an attribution error, missing condition, hidden bridge,
misclassified response, or scope problem and direct the student to a source or
rubric criterion. Do not write a replacement reconstruction, response,
judgment, or charity sentence. Apply course policy to fabricated citations or
concealed prohibited AI assistance rather than repairing them silently.
