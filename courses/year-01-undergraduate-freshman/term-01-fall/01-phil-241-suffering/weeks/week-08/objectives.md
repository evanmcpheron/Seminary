# Week 8 Objectives — The Evidential Problem of Evil

By the end of this week, the student will be able to:

1. distinguish a strict logical argument from an evidential argument and a
   deductively valid form from inductive support for a premise (`LO-01`,
   `LO-02`);
2. reconstruct Rowe's argument from intense suffering without silently
   changing its divine-attribute, greater-good, or worse-evil conditions
   (`LO-01`, `LO-02`);
3. explain why Rowe treats the isolated suffering of a fawn as evidence for a
   factual premise rather than as a deductive proof of that premise (`LO-02`);
4. distinguish *apparently gratuitous suffering* from *gratuitous suffering*
   and identify the inductive bridge between appearance and reality (`LO-01`,
   `LO-02`);
5. compare direct-theodicy, inference-challenge, counterevidence, moral-premise,
   and total-evidence replies without treating them as interchangeable
   (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
6. evaluate both the force and the limits of appeals to human cognitive
   limitation, reserving the full skeptical-theist debate for Week 10
   (`LO-02`, `LO-03`);
7. present Rowe's atheistic conclusion and his account of rationally justified
   theistic belief accurately and charitably before confessional evaluation
   (`LO-05`); and
8. produce a source-controlled dossier for comparing Rowe's argument with two
   materially different response strategies while withholding a final A-05
   thesis until the Week 9–10 evidence has been studied (`LO-02`, `LO-03`,
   `LO-05`).

No task requires personal disclosure or research into a real tragedy. A claim
about possible divine reasons never suspends a human duty to protect life,
report wrongdoing, relieve suffering, or seek appropriate care.
