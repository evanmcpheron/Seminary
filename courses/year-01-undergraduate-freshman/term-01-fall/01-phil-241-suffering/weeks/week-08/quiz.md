# Week 8 Retrieval Practice

**Status:** ungraded practice; not A-02 and worth no course points

**Time limit:** 15 minutes, including correction

Complete items 1–8 in nine minutes without books, notes, web, AI, or help.
Then open the instructor key and spend six minutes correcting every uncertain

## Closed-book retrieval — 9 minutes

1. Which statement best distinguishes the logical and evidential problems?

   - A. The logical problem concerns moral evil; the evidential problem
     concerns natural evil.
   - B. The logical problem alleges incompatibility; the evidential problem
     asks how observed evil bears on the reasonableness or probability of
     theism.
   - C. The logical problem uses arguments; the evidential problem uses only
     emotion.
   - D. The evidential problem begins only after atheism has been proved.

2. In Rowe's compact argument, which is the factual premise?

   - A. Perfect goodness is impossible.
   - B. No one can know anything about divine reasons.
   - C. Some intense suffering meets the greater-good and worse-evil gratuity
     conditions.
   - D. Every suffering produces a greater good.

3. Which statement is an appearance claim rather than a factual claim?

   - A. No qualifying justifying reason is apparent after responsible inquiry.
   - B. No qualifying justifying reason exists.
   - C. A perfectly good being would prevent gratuitous suffering.
   - D. Standard theism is false.

4. Why does Rowe's concession about possible unknown goods not end his
   evidential case?

   - A. Evidential support can be rational without certainty.
   - B. Unknown goods are logically impossible.
   - C. Rowe's argument is a strict contradiction.
   - D. The fawn is a divine being.

5. Which response is primarily undercutting?

   - A. Independent evidence proves theism.
   - B. A specified greater good actually requires this suffering.
   - C. Human failure to detect a reason is unreliable given the expected
     limits of human cognition.
   - D. Intense suffering is not bad.

6. What does a G. E. Moore shift do?

   - A. It shows Rowe's compact argument is invalid.
   - B. It begins from warranted theism, retains the qualified-prevention
     premise, and infers the denial of the factual premise.
   - C. It identifies God's reason for the fawn's suffering.
   - D. It denies that evidence matters.

7. In one sentence, distinguish evil as prima facie evidence from a verdict on
   total evidence.

8. In one sentence, explain why a possible hidden divine reason does not
   authorize a human agent to permit preventable harm.
