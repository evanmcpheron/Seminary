# Week 8 Required Reading — Rowe and the Evidential Inference

Use the approved student-owned print or personally licensed digital edition of
Peterson and the verified free official IEP entry. The Peterson assignment
identifies an exact verified chapter and a named contribution; it does not
invent a subrange where the student's print or digital edition may divide the
debate differently.

## Content notice and study boundary

The readings discuss severe human and animal suffering. Trakakis reports two
cases, one involving animal agony and another involving sexual violence and a
child's murder. Do not reproduce graphic details in any assignment. You may
pass over the extended narrative details of the latter case without penalty;
record only that it is presented as a case of horrendous moral evil and analyze
the argumentative role. No personal disclosure or outside tragedy research is
required.

## Reading questions

1. What is set aside by the debate question, and what evidence remains in
   view?
2. Why does Rowe's argument not allege a formal contradiction?
3. What makes a suffering instance gratuitous in the relevant sense?
4. Which condition concerns losing a greater good, and which concerns
   permitting an equally bad or worse evil?
5. Does the fawn case prove the factual premise? What does Rowe claim it does?
6. How do a particular case and the variety or profusion of suffering work
   together?
7. What is the difference between no known justifying good and no justifying
   good at all?
8. What assumption about human detection makes the move between those claims
   stronger or weaker?
9. Why is “there might be an unknown reason” not automatically a successful
   reply?
10. How can an atheist judge a theist rationally justified without judging
    theism true?
