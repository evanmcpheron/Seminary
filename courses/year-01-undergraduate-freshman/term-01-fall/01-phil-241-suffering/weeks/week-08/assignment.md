## A-01-J07 — Reading and Argument Journal 7

**Parent assessment:** A-01 Reading and argument journals

**Status:** graded, 10 points within the twelve-entry A-01 series

**Time budget:** 0.50 hour

**Outcomes:** `LO-01`, `LO-03`, `LO-05`

### Purpose

Explain how Rowe moves from apparently gratuitous suffering to an evidential
judgment, then compare one materially relevant response without caricature or
false certainty.

### Task

Write **300–400 words** under five headings:

1. **Rowe's claim:** State the relevant factual claim in your own words and
   locate it in Rowe's assigned Peterson contribution or in Trakakis section
   2. Preserve the greater-good and worse-evil conditions.
2. **Argumentative structure:** Distinguish the appearance claim, factual
   premise, theological premise, and conclusion. Explain why the overall case
   is evidential even though a compact form may be deductively valid.
3. **Inductive bridge:** Identify the assumption about human ability to detect
   a justifying good. State one reason Rowe's evidence has force and one limit
   on that force.
4. **Charitable response:** Present one direct-theodicy, inference-challenge,
   counterevidence, moral-premise, or target-revision response. Name the exact
   premise or inference addressed and whether the reply rebuts or undercuts.
5. **Provisional judgment:** State the strongest conclusion warranted and one
   stronger conclusion not yet warranted. Add one sentence explaining why a
   person holding the opposing position could still be rationally responsible.

Use paraphrase. A direct quotation is not required. If one is used, keep it
brief and cite the exact lawful-edition or stable web location. Do not reproduce
case details, research a real tragedy, or identify a hidden divine purpose.

Retain or submit one process document containing all of the following.

Copy the Week 7 provisional question, argument type, and response pair. Mark
each `retain`, `revise`, or `defer`, and give one evidence-based sentence for
every revision. If no Week 7 candidate is retained, preserve the earlier entry
in the change log rather than erasing the process.

#### 2. Source-location sheet

Do not cite the complete Peterson chapter for a claim made only by
Howard-Snyder or Bergmann, and do not treat Trakakis's exposition as Rowe's own

#### 3. Dual maps

- **Compact map:** state Rowe's factual premise, qualified-prevention premise,
  and conclusion. Test validity.
- **Expanded map:** add the appearance evidence and the inductive bridge to the
  factual premise. Label every statement not explicit in the selected source
  `student reconstruction`.

For each map, state the strongest conclusion and one conclusion it does not
establish.

#### 5. Two response profiles

Choose two responses that differ in central argumentative work, not merely in
author name. Available source-controlled families include a known-good or
theodicy response from Weeks 5–7, an inference challenge, a Moorean or
counterevidence response, or an explicitly identified target revision.

For each response, record:

1. the assigned and actually read source or lecture location;
2. the claim it addresses;
3. whether it rebuts, undercuts, supplies counterevidence, or revises the
   target;
4. its strongest reason;
5. one evidential burden it meets and one it leaves unmet; and
6. why it is materially different from the other response.

#### 6. Counterargument and limited reply

Give the strongest objection to the response currently judged more promising.
Name its point of contact: premise, bridge, source attribution, comparison,
scope, or moral implication. Then give a two- or three-sentence limited reply
that concedes what should be conceded and states what remains unresolved.

#### 7. Updated comparison architecture

Retain or revise three criteria for the parent paper. At least one must test
argumentative force and one must test conclusion scope. Record two questions
that require Week 9's Draper study or Week 10's skeptical-theism study. End
with a scheduled next action naming source, task, duration, and work block.
