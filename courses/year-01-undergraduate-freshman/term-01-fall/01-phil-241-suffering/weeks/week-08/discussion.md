# Week 8 Guided Discussion — When Does “I See No Reason” Count?

This is **30 minutes of ungraded guided practice**. Complete it with a partner
or as a recorded self-dialogue. Use only the hypothetical fawn case at the
level of detail stated in the lectures. No personal disclosure or outside case
research is permitted.

## Question

When, if ever, does a careful failure to identify a justifying divine reason
make it reasonable to believe that no such reason exists? Apply your answer to
Rowe's argument without claiming certainty.

## Roles

- **Rowean analyst:** defend the evidential force of the case and the variety
  or profusion of apparently gratuitous suffering. State the conclusion
  modestly.
- **Inference critic:** grant the evil and the absence of an apparent reason,
  then challenge detectability, competence, domain coverage, or the sample.
- **Scope and ethics keeper:** distinguish undercutting from rebutting, evil in
  isolation from total evidence, and divine permission from human duty.

For a self-dialogue, give each role a complete uninterrupted turn.

## Protocol

1. **Map — 5 minutes.** Jointly write the appearance claim, inductive bridge,
   factual premise, theological premise, and strongest proposed conclusion.
2. **Opening cases — 9 minutes.** The Rowean analyst and inference critic each
   speak for three minutes. The scope keeper uses three minutes to identify an
   overclaim from each side.
3. **Cross-examination — 8 minutes.** Ask each side two questions: one about
   expected detectability and one about conclusion scope. Answers must name a
   premise or inference.
4. **Role reversal — 4 minutes.** Each principal analyst states the strongest
   consideration learned from the other role.
5. **Joint verdict — 4 minutes.** Produce four sentences: evidence; bridge;
   defeater; scope. Add one human duty that remains clear under uncertainty.

## Completion standard

The exercise is complete when both positions are stated charitably, the
inductive bridge is explicit, at least one undercutting and one rebutting reply
are distinguished, the verdict avoids certainty and zero-evidence shortcuts,
and the final duty favors protection or care rather than passivity.
