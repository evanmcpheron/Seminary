# Week 9 Objectives — Probabilistic Arguments

By the end of Week 9, the student should be able to:

1. state Draper's theism, Hypothesis of Indifference, and observation report
   without replacing any of them with a broader or simpler claim (LO-01,
   LO-05);
2. explain why comparative explanatory power concerns how expected the evidence
   is under rival hypotheses rather than whether either hypothesis can merely
   accommodate the evidence (LO-01, LO-02);
3. reconstruct Draper's comparison of biologically useful and biologically
   gratuitous pain and pleasure among moral and nonmoral agents (LO-02,
   LO-05);
4. distinguish a likelihood comparison from a prior probability and an
   all-things-considered posterior judgment (LO-01, LO-02);
5. use a qualitative Bayesian update without inventing numerical probabilities
   or treating a likelihood advantage as a deductive proof (LO-01, LO-02);
6. explain how a theodicy functions as an expansion of theism and why its
   internal plausibility and predictive fit both affect its evidential value
   (LO-02, LO-03);
7. compare Rowe's inference from apparently gratuitous suffering with Draper's
   distributional and hypothesis-comparative argument (LO-02, LO-03, LO-05);
8. present a serious theistic response and Draper's strongest reply before
   offering a bounded confessional judgment (LO-03, LO-05); and
9. preserve ordinary duties of protection, relief, reporting, and care while
   philosophical questions about divine permission remain disputed (LO-03,
   LO-05).

These objectives implement the approved Week 9 schedule. They do not authorize
new required sources, an alteration of the parent A-05 paper, or production of
Week 10.
