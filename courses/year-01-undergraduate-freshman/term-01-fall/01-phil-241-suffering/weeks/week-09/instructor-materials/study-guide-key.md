# Instructor Key — Week 9 Study Guide

Use this key after the student completes and self-corrects the ungraded guide.
Accept equivalent wording that preserves conditional direction, source voice,
and conclusion scope.

## Part 1 — Core vocabulary

1. **HI:** neither the nature nor condition of earthly sentient beings results
   from benevolent or malevolent nonhuman action. Contrast: it does not entail
   naturalism or deny every supernatural being.
2. **O:** the inquirer's observations and encountered testimony about human and
   animal pain and pleasure. Contrast: it is not an omniscient census.
3. **Antecedent epistemic probability:** rational probability assessed
   independently of O's observations and testimony. Contrast: antecedent does
   not mean earlier in time.
4. **Biological usefulness:** nonaccidental contribution to a biological goal
   of an organic system. Contrast: biological function is not identical with
   moral justification.
5. **Biologically gratuitous:** not contributing to a biological goal in
   Draper's sense. Contrast: morally gratuitous means lacking a morally
   sufficient reason for permission.
6. **Likelihood:** P(E given H), asking how expected evidence is on a
   hypothesis. Contrast: not P(H given E).
7. **Prior:** rational support for H before the present E is incorporated.
   Contrast: it must not include E and then count E again.
8. **Posterior:** rational support for H after updating on E. Contrast: it is
   not supplied by a likelihood alone.

## Part 2 — O1/O2/O3 map

| Component | Subjects | Kind | Draper's comparison | Main reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | moral agents | pain and pleasure known to be biologically useful | much more probable on HI than theism | biological pattern is expected on HI; a good omnipotent creator has moral reasons and alternatives not tied automatically to that pattern |
| O2 given O1 | sentient nonmoral beings | pain and pleasure known to be biologically useful | somewhat more probable on HI than theism | biological similarity predicts shared systems on HI, while moral-agency reasons do not transfer straightforwardly |
| O3 given O1 and O2 | sentient beings | pain and pleasure not known to be biologically useful | much more probable on HI than theism | pathological and biologically appropriate spillover fit a fundamentally biological system, while expected happiness and moral correlation pressure theism |

Do not require the exact comparative adjectives if the student preserves
Draper's ordering and does not invent numerical ratios.

## Part 3 — Probability roles

1. Likelihood.
2. Prior.
3. Posterior comparison.
4. Total-evidence judgment.

Moving from 1 to 3 requires priors and the rival likelihoods. Moving from 3 to
4 requires the remaining relevant evidence, its objections, and dependence
audit. No percentage follows without a justified probability model.

## Part 4 — Expansion audit

Responses vary. Check these anchors:

- T1 adds a divine aim involving a favorable balance of freely performed right
  actions; it predicts some pain connected to right action but faces pain's
  contribution to wrongdoing and demoralizing conditions.
- T2 adds a divine aim involving freedom for very important moral decisions; it
  predicts serious human-caused harm but faces amoral pain, alternative means
  of knowledge, amount, and worthiness for grave responsibility.
- T3 adds divine knowledge vastly beyond ours; it predicts human explanatory
  limitation but, in Draper's reply, unknown knowledge could favor prevention
  or less painful means as well as permission.

An expansion normally attempts to change P(O given theism). A stronger
skeptical-theist version may undercut confidence in the comparison itself, but
its full evaluation belongs to Week 10.

## Part 5 — Rowe/Draper comparison

- Rowe: apparently gratuitous cases or their profusion; bridge from no known
  justifying reason to probable absence; conclusion about probable gratuity
  and, with the moral premise, atheism.
- Draper: the distribution of pain and pleasure; bridge from comparative
  likelihood to evidential favoring; conclusion that O favors HI and creates a
  prima facie problem.

Animal suffering and apparent lack of adequate purpose are possible overlaps.
Do not count them as independent merely because two argument maps use them.
The full cognitive-limit and moral-skepticism questions are deferred to Week
10.

## Correction threshold

The practice is complete when every part has been attempted from memory,
corrections are visible, HI is not equated with naturalism, biological and
moral gratuity remain distinct, conditionals are not reversed, and no
unsupported probability or token divine purpose remains.
