# A-02-Q04 — Retrieval Quiz 4

This is a **graded, cumulative, closed-book quiz** emphasizing Weeks 8–9 while
retaining foundational distinctions from Weeks 1–7. Complete it after all
other Week 9 work.

## Conditions

- **Time:** 30 minutes total
- **Materials:** closed book, closed notes, no web, no AI, no assistance
- **Format:** eight multiple-choice items and two short analysis items
- **Points:** 20 total
- **Submission:** submit answers and the integrity statement when time ends

Spend about 10 minutes on Part I, 17 minutes on Part II, and 3 minutes checking
completion and signing the statement. Do not consult instructor materials.

## Part I — Concepts and distinctions, 8 points

Select the best answer. Each item is worth 1 point.

1. Which statement best represents Draper's Hypothesis of Indifference?

   A. No supernatural being of any kind exists.
   B. Nature is governed by a malevolent creator.
   C. Neither the nature nor condition of earthly sentient beings results from
   benevolent or malevolent nonhuman action.
   D. Every pain lacks a biological function.

2. Draper's observation report O includes:

   A. only apparently gratuitous human pain;
   B. observations and encountered testimony about human and animal pain and
   pleasure;
   C. all facts in the universe known by an omniscient observer;
   D. only experiences known to lack biological usefulness.

3. In Draper's argument, antecedent probability means probability:

   A. measured before 1989;
   B. calculated by counting repeated universes;
   C. assessed independently of the observations and testimony O reports;
   D. fixed identically for every inquirer regardless of evidence.

4. Which distinction is accurate?

   A. Biologically gratuitous pain is automatically morally gratuitous pain.
   B. Biologically appropriate pain must help the individual in that token
   event.
   C. Pathological pain results from proper organic functioning.
   D. A pain may lack biological usefulness while a proposed moral or
   theological justification remains a separate question.

5. What follows directly if P(O given HI) is greater than P(O given theism)?

   A. O favors HI over theism, other relevant probabilistic questions still to
   be considered.
   B. HI is certainly true.
   C. Naturalism is deductively established.
   D. Every theist is irrational.

6. According to Draper's use of the Weighted Average Principle, a theistic
expansion most improves P(O given theism) when it:

   A. is merely logically possible;
   B. is sufficiently probable given theism and makes O more expected;
   C. repeats O inside its definition after the evidence is known;
   D. assigns a numerical probability without a model.

7. Which comparison between Rowe and Draper is most accurate?

   A. Both arguments depend on a strict contradiction between God and evil.
   B. Rowe infers probable gratuity from apparent gratuity; Draper compares the
   likelihood of a distribution under rival hypotheses.
   C. Draper argues that every token suffering is gratuitous; Rowe denies that
   suffering is evidence.
   D. Rowe's and Draper's animal evidence is automatically independent.

8. Which response is counterevidence rather than a direct likelihood reply?

   A. O2 does not concern nonmoral sentient beings.
   B. HI is logically identical with naturalism.
   C. Independent historical evidence supports the morally perfect creator
   strongly enough to offset O's local evidential force.
   D. A biological warning system has no organic function.

## Part II — Short analysis, 12 points

Answer each in no more than 150 words. Each item is worth 6 points.

9. A theist says, “Pain warns organisms about damage, so Draper's argument
   fails.” Explain why biological usefulness is part of Draper's evidence
   rather than an automatic answer. Your response must state the role of O1,
   add the pressure introduced by either O2 or O3, and identify one specific
   way a theistic expansion would need to improve the comparison.

10. A student writes, “O is more likely on HI than on theism. Therefore
    naturalism is certainly true, priors and other evidence are irrelevant,
    and Rowe independently proves the same conclusion with the same animal
    evidence.” Identify **four distinct probability, scope, or dependence
    errors**. Correct the likelihood conclusion, state where priors or
    counterevidence enter, and explain the double-counting risk.
