# Lecture 1 Outline — Draper on the Distribution of Pain and Pleasure

## Objectives

- Define Draper's rival hypotheses and observation report precisely.
- Explain explanatory comparison through relative expectedness.
- Reconstruct the three-part distributional analysis of pain and pleasure.
- State the argument's prima facie conclusion and its limits.

## Outline

1. From Rowe's cases to Draper's distribution
2. Theism, the Hypothesis of Indifference, and observation O
3. Explanation as comparative surprise
4. Antecedent epistemic probability
5. Biological usefulness and intrinsic moral value
6. O1: moral agents and biologically useful pain and pleasure
7. O2: nonmoral sentient beings
8. O3: pain and pleasure not known to be biologically useful
9. Pathological and biologically appropriate experiences
10. From the likelihood comparison to a prima facie challenge
11. Strongest pressures and confessional reception
12. Moral and pastoral boundaries

Theism; Hypothesis of Indifference; observation report O; explanatory power;
comparative surprise; antecedent epistemic probability; biological usefulness;
biologically gratuitous; pathological; biologically appropriate; moral agent;
