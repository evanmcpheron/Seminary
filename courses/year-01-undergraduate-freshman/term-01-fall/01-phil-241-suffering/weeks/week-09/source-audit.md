## Draper terminology and target audit

- Theism is represented as the existence of an omnipotent, omniscient, morally
  perfect person who created the universe.
- HI is represented as a claim about the absence of benevolent or malevolent
  nonhuman action explaining the nature or condition of earthly sentient
  beings. It is not equated with naturalism, atheism in every sense, hostility,
  or denial of every supernatural being.
- O includes observations of human and animal pain and pleasure and encountered
  testimony about such observations. It is not described as an exhaustive
  empirical census.
- Antecedent probability is correctly explained as probability assessed
  independently of the observations and testimony O reports, not temporal
  priority.
- Draper's probabilities are identified as epistemic rather than statistical,
  physical, or logical probabilities.
- Biological usefulness requires a nonaccidental contribution to a biological
  goal. Biological goal-directedness is not treated as conscious intention.
- Biologically gratuitous pain is explicitly distinguished from Rowe's morally
  gratuitous suffering.
- Pathological experience is tied to organic malfunction, while biologically
  appropriate experience can occur in a type of situation where the response
  is generally useful even when the token does not aid the subject.

## Distributional and probability audit

- The central conclusion is accurately stated as P(O given HI) being much
  greater than P(O given theism), not as the logical impossibility of theism.
- Explanatory advantage is tied to comparative expectedness or surprise.
  Possibility and accommodation are not confused with prediction.
- O1 concerns biologically useful pain and pleasure among moral agents; Draper
  judges it much more probable on HI than on theism.
- O2 concerns biologically useful pain and pleasure among sentient nonmoral
  beings; conditioned on O1, Draper judges it somewhat more probable on HI.
- O3 concerns pain and pleasure not known to be biologically useful; conditioned
  on O1 and O2, Draper judges it much more probable on HI.
- The lectures preserve conditional ordering and warn against treating related
  observations as independent.
- Likelihood, prior probability, and posterior probability are separated.
  Standard Bayesian relations are labeled as curriculum synthesis rather than
  Draper's exact pedagogical presentation.
- No numerical probability, likelihood ratio, prior, or posterior is invented.
- Draper's prima facie epistemic conclusion is reported without converting it
  into proof, naturalism, or a verdict about every theist's rationality.

## Theodicy and response audit

- An expansion is correctly represented as a more detailed statement known to
  entail theism.
- WAP correctly partitions theism into the expansion and its negation and
  weights each likelihood by the expansion's probability given theism.
- The lectures draw the correct dual test: an expansion must have sufficient
  probability given theism and must make O more expected than the relevant
  alternative within theism.
- T1 is connected to a favorable balance of freely performed right actions.
  Its predictive successes and demoralizing or wrongdoing-related liabilities
  are both represented.
- T2 is connected to freedom to make very important moral decisions. Draper's
  objections concerning amoral pain, less costly knowledge, amount, and
  agents' worthiness for grave responsibility are preserved without extended
  examples.
- T3 is connected to divine knowledge of good and evil far beyond human
  knowledge. Draper's reply that unknown divine knowledge may include
  pain-avoiding goods or means as well as pain-requiring reasons is accurately
  stated.
- The lectures acknowledge but do not preempt the stronger skeptical-theist
  challenge assigned to Week 10.
- The comparison-class objection, Christian expansion, and total-evidence
  response are marked as contested responses rather than conclusions Draper
  accepts.

## Rowe comparison and conclusion-scope audit

- Rowe's evidence unit remains apparently gratuitous intense suffering;
  Draper's evidence unit is a distributional observation report about pain and
  pleasure.
- Rowe's contested bridge moves from no known justifying good toward probable
  absence of such a good. Draper's bridge moves from comparative likelihood to
  evidential favoring.
- Draper does not need to establish that a particular suffering is morally
  gratuitous for his central likelihood comparison.
- The arguments are permitted to reinforce one another, but overlapping animal
  and suffering evidence is flagged for dependence and possible double
  counting.
- Local evidential favoring is separated from posterior preference and a
  total-evidence worldview conclusion.

## Claim-label, confessional, and biblical audit

- Primary-source claims, broad probability distinctions, contested judgments,
  confessional conclusions, and curriculum synthesis are explicitly signaled
  where their roles could be confused.
- The skeptical argument is presented in a strong form before confessional
  evaluation. HI is not mocked, reduced to motive, or described as moral
  hostility.
- Christian evaluation retains the divine attributes Draper targets rather
  than solving the problem by silently adopting a finite or indifferent deity.
- Genesis 1–3, Mark 15, Romans 8:18–39, and Revelation 21–22 are used at the
  level of canonical themes already audited in prior weeks. No new
  original-language, translation, date, or authorship claim is introduced.
- Canonical synthesis is allowed to contribute to a thicker Christian
  hypothesis, but it is not assigned a numerical probability or treated as a
  token explanation.
- The lectures concede that biological function does not by itself establish
  moral justification, necessity, or defeat.

## Pastoral, safety, and copyright audit

- The Week 9 reading guide gives a content notice and does not require graphic
  retelling, personal disclosure, diagnosis, or outside tragedy research.
- Classroom categories are not recommended as labels to impose on a sufferer.
- Divine permission is separated from creaturely duty and human culpability.
  No possible divine reason excuses abuse, negligence, nonreporting, or failure
  to seek appropriate care.
- Immediate safety, qualified medical and mental-health care, safeguarding,
  reporting, justice, and practical aid remain intact under uncertainty.
- The lectures and outlines are original synthesis. No extended passage from
  Draper, Peterson, Rowe, Trakakis, or a copyrighted Bible translation is
  reproduced. No source copy is stored in the repository.
