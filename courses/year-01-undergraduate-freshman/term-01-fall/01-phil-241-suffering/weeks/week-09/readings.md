# Week 9 Required Reading — Draper and Explanatory Comparison

Use the approved student-owned print or personally licensed digital edition of
Peterson. The Week 9 selection has been verified against the second-edition
JSTOR table of contents. Review only the specified Week 8 materials; no new
purchase or external reading is required.

## Content notice and study boundary

The assigned sources discuss physical and mental pain, nonhuman animal
suffering, illness, poverty, serious wrongdoing, and failures of moral agents.
Analyze argument structure and distributional claims without compiling graphic
examples. No personal disclosure, diagnosis, or outside tragedy research is
required.

## Reading questions

1. What exactly does Draper's theism assert?
2. Why is HI not identical with naturalism or with the simple denial of theism?
3. What observations and testimony are included in O?
4. In this argument, what does it mean for O to be less surprising on one
   hypothesis than another?
5. Why does antecedent mean independent of O rather than earlier in time?
6. What makes pain or pleasure biologically useful?
7. How do O1, O2, and O3 divide the evidence?
8. Why does Draper regard the biologically patterned distribution as more
   expected on HI?
9. What is the difference between biologically gratuitous and morally
   gratuitous pain?
10. How can an experience be biologically appropriate without helping the
    individual on that occasion?
11. What must a theodicy do to raise the probability of O on theism?
12. Why does a likelihood comparison not by itself settle the posterior
    probability of theism?
13. How does Draper's method differ from Rowe's case-based inference?
14. What counterevidence could matter without making pain good or unreal?
