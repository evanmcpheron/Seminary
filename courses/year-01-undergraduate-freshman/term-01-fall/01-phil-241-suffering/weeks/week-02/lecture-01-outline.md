# Lecture 1 Outline — Creation, Fall, and Lament

## Objectives

- Trace creation, rebellion, curse, exile, and lament without assigning every
  sufferer's pain to a particular sin.
- Distinguish direct textual claims from later theological inference.
- Compare Psalms 22, 73, and 88 as distinct forms of faithful protest.
- Reconstruct and critically assess Lewis's account of divine goodness and love.

## Outline

1. A biblical grammar rather than a one-cause formula
2. Genesis 1–2: created goodness, embodied dependence, vocation, and limit
3. Genesis 3: mistrust, transgression, blame, fractured relations, and exile
4. What the fall framework explains—and what it does not authorize us to say
5. Lament as covenant address rather than pious denial
6. Psalm 22: complaint, remembered trust, petition, and praise
7. Psalm 73: moral disorder re-seen before God
8. Psalm 88: prayer without narrated resolution
9. Lewis on goodness, kindness, love, and limited analogy
10. Confessional synthesis and rules for pastoral use

## Key terms

Creation; goodness; image of God; vocation; creaturely limit; fall; sin; curse;
alienation; exile; lament; protest; retribution; moral order; analogy; divine
goodness; kindness; perfecting love; pastoral misuse.
