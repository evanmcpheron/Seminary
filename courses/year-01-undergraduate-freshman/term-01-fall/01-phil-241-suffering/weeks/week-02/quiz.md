# A-02 Retrieval Quiz 1 — Weeks 1–2

This is a **graded, cumulative, closed-book, timed quiz**. It is the first of six
A-02 quizzes and contributes one-sixth of the A-02 quiz grade. Once begun, use
no notes, books, Bible, lecture files, internet resources, another person, or AI.
Stop after 30 minutes and submit the work completed. No personal disclosure is
required.

## Instructions

- Answer every item concisely in your own words.
- For selected-response items, record the letter and the key phrase.
- For short answers, use complete sentences or a clearly labeled argument map.
- Do not consult the separate instructor key.

## Part I — Selected response, 6 points

Choose the best answer. Each item is worth 1 point.

1. Which statement best distinguishes an evidential problem of evil from a
   logical problem?

   A. It denies that suffering exists.

   B. It argues that actual evils count against the probability or
   reasonableness of theism without requiring a strict contradiction.

   C. It gives God's actual reason for each evil.

   D. It concerns feelings rather than reasons.

2. A proposal that claims to identify God's actual morally sufficient reasons
   for permitting evil is best classified as:

   A. a defense; B. a lament; C. a theodicy; D. a contradiction.

3. Which assigned psalm ends without a narrated turn to deliverance or praise?

   A. Psalm 22; B. Psalm 73; C. Psalm 88; D. none of them.

4. Which claim is ruled out by the prologue's characterization of Job?

   A. Job experiences severe suffering.

   B. Job lacks access to the heavenly scene.

   C. Job's losses prove that he committed a severe hidden sin.

   D. Job's friends first sit with him in silence.

5. In the immediate sequence of Romans 8:28–29, the promised good is shaped by:

   A. immediate comfort; B. conformity to God's Son; C. escape from embodiment;
   D. knowledge of every event's purpose.

6. Which pair belongs explicitly to the hope depicted in Revelation 21–22?

   A. denial and forgetting; B. disembodied escape and secrecy; C. divine
   presence and the end of death; D. human mastery and the end of judgment.

## Part II — Concise source and concept answers, 8 points

Answer in two or three sentences each. Each item is worth 2 points.

7. Give one direct claim of Genesis 1–3 and one related theological inference.
   Explain why the two should be labeled differently.

8. State one fact the reader of Job 1–2 knows that Job's friends do not. Explain
   how that fact limits their diagnosis.

9. Distinguish Lewis's account of kindness from his account of love in chapter
   3, then state one limitation of his analogies.

10. Identify the threefold groaning in Romans 8 and state what it shows about
    the relation between present suffering and Christian hope.

## Part III — Argument structure, 6 points

11. Reconstruct the friends' simple retributive diagnosis of Job as a numbered
    argument with two or three premises and a conclusion. Then identify the
    premise undermined by Job 1–2 and explain how God's verdict in Job 42 bears
    on the argument.

## Student-facing scoring standard

See the A-02-Q01 section of [rubric.md](rubric.md). Credit is awarded for
accurate retrieval, precise distinctions, valid premise-conclusion structure,
and source-controlled explanation. Confessional agreement by itself neither
earns nor loses credit.
