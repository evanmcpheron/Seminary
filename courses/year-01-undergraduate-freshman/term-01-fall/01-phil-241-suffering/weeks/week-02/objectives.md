# Week 2 Objectives — Biblical Grammar for Suffering

By the end of this week, the student will be able to:

1. trace the canonical movement from good creation through creaturely rebellion,
   lament, judgment, and new-creation hope without reducing it to a single cause
   for every instance of suffering (`LO-01`, `LO-04`);
2. distinguish what Genesis 1–3 says directly from later theological inferences
   about moral evil, natural disorder, pain, toil, death, and exile (`LO-01`,
   `LO-04`);
3. compare Psalms 22, 73, and 88 as different faithful forms of lament, protest,
   moral perplexity, remembrance, and hope (`LO-04`, `LO-07`);
4. explain how Job resists a simple retributive equation between suffering and a
   sufferer's guilt while preserving questions about providence, creaturely
   limits, justice, and truthful speech about God (`LO-01`, `LO-04`);
5. relate Romans 8:18–39 and Revelation 21–22 to present groaning, divine love,
   judgment, resurrection, and new creation without calling evil good or
   claiming knowledge of God's purpose in a particular tragedy (`LO-04`,
   `LO-07`);
6. reconstruct Lewis's chapter 3 account of divine goodness and love, identify
   the work done by his analogies, and state a serious objection to their use
   (`LO-01`, `LO-04`); and
7. use biblical texts in a synthetic pastoral exchange with contextual care,
   non-coercive language, and respect for the speaker's agency (`LO-07`).

No objective requires the student to disclose personal suffering or to identify
a divine reason for a particular person's experience.
