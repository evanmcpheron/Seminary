# Week 2 Guided Discussion — Lament, Explanation, and Hope

This is 75 minutes of ungraded oral practice. Use the fictional case below; do
not substitute personal trauma or another person's private information. The aim
is accurate listening, contextual use of Scripture, charitable disagreement,
and non-coercive pastoral judgment—not a performance of counseling.

If no partner or group is available, record the timed responses privately,
listen once, and complete the same debrief. The recording is not submitted.

## Fictional case

After a tornado destroys an apartment building, Sam says to a church discussion
group:

> “People keep telling me everything happens for a reason and quoting Romans
> 8:28. Some residents died, and others lost everything. If God governs this,
> why should anyone call God good? I do not want an explanation that blames the
> people who were hurt.”

Sam reports no immediate danger and asks to discuss the theological question.
If a real participant discloses danger, abuse, or risk of harm, stop the exercise
and follow the course's safety and referral policy.

## 1. Individual source preparation — 15 minutes

Prepare four index-card-sized notes:

1. a charitable statement of Sam's philosophical and moral challenge;
2. one relevant insight from Job and one claim Job does **not** authorize;
3. Romans 8:28 within the paragraph's sequence of groaning, hope, Christ-shaped
   good, and inseparable love; and
4. one use of a lament psalm or Revelation 21–22 that preserves context and does
   not pressure Sam toward a predetermined emotional response.

## 2. Structured dialogue — 40 minutes

Assign a facilitator/timekeeper. Rotate the roles of **critical reader**,
**Christian respondent**, and **text auditor**. In a pair, alternate the first
two roles and jointly perform the audit.

### Round A: represent before replying — 10 minutes

The critical reader develops Sam's challenge without caricature. The Christian
respondent paraphrases it and asks whether the representation is fair. The text
auditor notes any move from an event's occurrence to a claim about its cause,
purpose, goodness, or the victims' guilt.

### Round B: Job and providence — 10 minutes

The respondent uses Job to offer a bounded theological answer. The critical
reader presses two questions: What makes divine permission morally different
from indifference? What does the divine speech explain about this tornado? The
respondent must identify what remains unresolved.

### Round C: Romans and Revelation — 10 minutes

The respondent places Romans 8:28 in context and relates it to Revelation
21–22. The critical reader asks whether future healing can justify present
suffering. Distinguish hope for evil's defeat from a claim that the evil was
good or necessary.

### Round D: first pastoral response — 10 minutes

Compose a response of no more than four sentences. It must:

- acknowledge the stated loss and the legitimacy of the question;
- avoid blame and hidden-purpose claims;
- ask consent before extended scriptural or philosophical discussion; and
- preserve hope without demanding emotional resolution.

The text auditor checks each sentence against the audited lectures and course
pastoral boundaries.

## 3. Debrief and revision — 20 minutes

Discuss these questions:

1. Where did a general theological claim nearly become a case-specific claim?
2. Which opposing question was hardest to answer fairly?
3. Did any biblical reference function as evidence, orientation, lament, or
   conversational closure? Was that use appropriate?
4. What remains philosophically unresolved after the canonical synthesis?
