# Instructor Key — A-02 Retrieval Quiz 1

Secure instructor material. Do not distribute before all students have completed
the quiz. This key supplies grading anchors, not language that must be reproduced
verbatim. Substantively equivalent accurate answers receive credit.

## Part I — 6 points

1. **B** — Evidential arguments can claim that actual evil counts against the
   probability or reasonableness of theism without alleging strict contradiction.
2. **C** — A theodicy proposes God's actual morally sufficient reasons; a
   defense need claim only a relevant possibility.
3. **C** — Psalm 88 ends without narrated deliverance or a turn to praise.
4. **C** — The narrator characterizes Job as blameless and upright; the losses
   do not prove a severe hidden sin.
5. **B** — Romans 8:29 specifies conformity to God's Son.
6. **C** — Revelation 21–22 joins divine presence with the end of death.

Award 1 point only for the correct response. The requested key phrase may be
abbreviated if the letter is unambiguous.

## Part II — 8 points

### Item 7 — 2 points

Award 1 point for any accurate direct claim from Genesis 1–3, such as God's
evaluation of creation as good, humanity's image-bearing vocation, the command,
the transgression, or the narrated judgments and exile. Award 1 point for a
related inference that is labeled as inference and for explaining that it
relates or systematizes textual claims rather than reproducing a sentence in the
passage. A response that presents personal suffering as proof of personal sin
does not earn the inference point.

### Item 8 — 2 points

Award 1 point for either of these reader-only controls:

- Job is introduced as blameless/upright, and no severe hidden sin is given as
  the cause; or
- the reader sees the heavenly accusation and permitted test that Job and the
  friends do not see.

Award 1 point for explaining that the friends therefore cannot infer Job's
hidden guilt from his suffering. Merely saying “the friends are wrong” earns no
second point without the knowledge-to-inference connection.

### Item 9 — 2 points

Award 1 point for distinguishing mere kindness, understood by Lewis as wanting
another's contentment on the other's current terms, from love that seeks the
beloved's actual good and transformation. Award 1 point for a relevant limit:
creator-creature relation is unique; each analogy illuminates only an aspect;
human unequal relationships can be abusive; an artist's relation to a nonperson
cannot justify treatment of persons; or perfecting love does not by itself
justify a particular horror.

### Item 10 — 2 points

Award 1 point only if all three are named: creation, believers, and the Spirit.
Award 1 point for explaining that groaning occurs within, rather than disproves,
hope: creation and bodies await liberation/redemption, and the Spirit aids
weakness. Do not require confessional assent; require accurate representation of
the passage.

## Part III — 6 points

### Item 11

An acceptable map is:

1. Severe suffering occurs only when God punishes the sufferer's severe sin.
2. Job is suffering severely.
3. Therefore, Job has committed severe sin and should confess it.

Equivalent two- or three-premise maps are acceptable. Score as follows:

- **2 points — structure:** premises and conclusion are distinguishable, and
  the conclusion follows if the premises are granted;
- **2 points — failed premise:** identifies the universal punishment premise as
  false or unavailable in Job's case and uses the prologue's characterization
  or hidden test to explain why; and
- **2 points — Job 42:** explains that God says the friends have not spoken
  rightly about God as Job has and requires sacrifice and Job's intercession,
  thereby refusing their diagnostic speech.

Deduct 1 point from the applicable category if the response says the friends'
general concern for divine justice is itself the only error but never identifies
the invalid case-specific diagnosis. A response may critically question the
book and still earn full credit when it represents its narrative controls
accurately.
