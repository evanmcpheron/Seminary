# Lecture 2 Outline — Job, Providence, Judgment, and Hope

## Objectives

- Explain how Job disrupts a simple equation of suffering with personal guilt.
- Distinguish the reader's knowledge, Job's knowledge, and the friends' claims.
- Relate creaturely limits to providence without making power a substitute for
  goodness or explanation.
- Trace Romans 8 and Revelation 21–22 from groaning through judgment to renewed
  creation and inseparable divine love.

## Outline

1. Job as a test of speech about suffering
2. The prologue: integrity, hidden causes, loss, lament, and presence
3. The friends' retributive logic and the danger of misapplied truth
4. The divine speeches: knowledge, governance, wild creation, and creaturely
   location
5. Job's response, God's verdict, and restoration that does not erase loss
6. Providence: what Job warrants and what it leaves unresolved
7. Romans 8: creation's groaning, the Spirit's groaning, hope, and Christ-shaped
   good
8. Revelation 21–22: judgment, presence, healed creation, and the end of curse
9. Christian hope without minimization or speculative explanation
10. Pastoral rules for speaking Scripture in the presence of suffering

## Key terms

Retribution principle; integrity; providence; creaturely limitation; divine
speech; epistemic humility; groaning; hope; resurrection; conformity to Christ;
judgment; new creation; divine presence; curse; restoration.
